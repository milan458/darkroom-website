What should a good photo vault app do?

Encrypt your files on the iPhone, back them up somewhere that restores, and let you back in if you forget your code. Features matter less than these.

  1. Real encryption, on your device. A PIN screen in front of normal files is a lock on the front door with the back door open. Look for a named cipher such as AES-256, and for encryption that happens on the iPhone before anything is uploaded. Our guide to how encrypted photo vaults work explains the difference between locking and encrypting.
  2. A backup that restores. Ask where the backup lives (your iCloud or the developer's servers), who holds the keys, and how you restore on a new iPhone. SPV's listing, for one, says that if you delete the app, the photos in it are deleted too.
  3. A way back in. If nobody can reset your code, you need a recovery phrase or key you keep yourself. If the developer can reset it by email, they can also, in principle, get in. See forgot your photo vault password.
  4. A decoy, if someone may make you open it. A second code that opens a harmless vault helps only if that vault looks lived in. See what a decoy vault is and its limits.
  5. Honest pricing and privacy. Check whether ads or a lapsed subscription can lock your photos, and read the App Store privacy label. Vaults you can buy once are in vaults without subscriptions or ads.

What no vault protects. No vault, Noxium included, protects photos while they still sit in your Photos library before you import them, or copies you have already sent to someone. A drawn pattern can be watched over your shoulder, like a PIN. And every claim on this page is the developer's own: each App Store page notes that its privacy details have not been verified by Apple.

What is each vault app good and bad at?

One line per app, strengths first, from what its own listing says. Where we've written a full review, the app's name links to it, and all our reviews are in Noxium compared with other photo vaults.

  • Private Photo Vault: the most-rated app in this table, with a decoy password and an encrypted Cloud Vault on paid plans. Its listing names no cipher, the free version doesn't back up, and it lists Usage Data used to track you.
  • Keepsafe: very popular, with 256-bit AES named in its help center, a fake PIN decoy with Premium, and its own cloud. Its account is tied to a verified email address, and without access to that email the account can't be recovered (help center). The free version, Keepsafe Basic, is ad supported.
  • Privault: a second passcode opens a second folder and there is a $45.99 Permanent Pro, but the listing describes no backup and the app hasn't been updated since February 2025.
  • SPV: widely used and keeps nothing on a server, but the listing doesn't state encryption or a decoy, says deleting the app deletes its photos, and lists diagnostics, identifiers and usage data used to track you.
  • Secret Photo Album (SA): a decoy PIN and cloud backup with Premium, with no cipher named and the longest list of tracking data in the table.
  • PV – Secret Photo Album: a fake password, a $41.99 permanent membership and its own cloud on paid tiers; the listing says data is encrypted but doesn't say how, and the app contains ads.
  • Calculator#: a calculator disguise with a fake crash screen and backup to four cloud services, but no encryption stated and ads in the free version.
  • Secret Photo Vault – Lock Photos: decoy accounts, a single $6.99 Pro purchase and Data Not Collected, but no encryption named, no cloud backup described and no update since April 2025.
  • Safe Lock: a fake password opens a fake gallery and the label is Data Not Collected; the listing doesn't state encryption.
  • Hide it Pro: a simple hider with a fake crash screen, paid Cloud Storage and a paid Password Reset; no encryption stated.
  • KYMS: a fake PIN opens a decoy vault and the listing says "AES military-grade", but it describes no backup and sells ad removal for $4.99.
  • MaxVault: a lifetime purchase and links to iCloud Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive; the listing names no encryption and no decoy.
  • HiddenVault: a month's free trial and a "Zero-Knowledge" claim, but no cipher, backup or decoy described, and subscriptions only.
  • Ente Photos: open source, third-party audited and end-to-end encrypted, with 10 GB free, but it's a cloud photo library with an account, not a hidden vault.
  • Encamera: open-source code, no account, no ads, and optional storage in your own iCloud with Premium; the listing names no decoy.
  • Vaultaire: AES-256-GCM, a pattern lock, a decoy with a duress vault in Pro, and a free encrypted iCloud backup. New, with 72 ratings, and no recovery described.
  • Obscura: AES-256-GCM with open-source encryption code, a 24-word recovery key, and no cloud of its own. The free plan holds only 50 items.
  • Apple's Hidden album: free and built in, locked by default, but anyone with your passcode can open it.

Questions people ask

What is the best photo vault app for iPhone?

The one that names its encryption, backs up somewhere you control, and gives you a way back in. Of the apps from other developers, Obscura's listing claims all three and Vaultaire's claims the first two. Noxium, which we make, does all three. Which suits you depends on backup, decoys and price.

What is the best free photo vault for iPhone?

Apple's Hidden album is free and built in. Among vault apps, compare free plans: Noxium's holds 150 photos and 10 videos with encrypted iCloud backup, Vaultaire's holds 100 photos and 10 videos with encrypted iCloud backup, and Obscura's holds 50 photos and documents.

Is there a picture vault built into iPhone?

Yes, the Hidden album in Photos, under Collections, then Utilities. It's locked by default since iOS 16 and opens with Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode. It's not a separate vault: anyone who knows your iPhone passcode can open it. Read is the Hidden album private?

What is the best encryption app for iPhone photos?

Look for one that names its cipher and encrypts on the iPhone. Vaultaire and Obscura name AES-256-GCM in their listings, and Obscura's encryption code is open source. Noxium, which we make, uses AES-256-GCM with keys from Argon2id. Ente Photos is end-to-end encrypted and audited, but it's a cloud library, not a vault.

Do photo vault apps actually encrypt photos?

Not all of them say so. Of the 17 listings from other developers we checked, 6 don't mention encryption at all. See how encrypted photo vaults work for the difference between locking and encrypting.
