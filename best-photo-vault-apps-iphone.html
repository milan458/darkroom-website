The best photo vault apps for iPhone in 2026, compared

We compared 17 photo vault apps for iPhone and Apple's Hidden album on what actually protects your photos: whether the developer says how files are encrypted, how backups work, whether there's a decoy vault, what the app tracks, and what it costs.

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 18 November 2026

Short answer

Pick a vault that names its encryption, keeps an encrypted backup you control, and gives you a way back in if you forget your code. Of the 17 apps we checked on 22 October 2026, only two name AES-256-GCM in their App Store description (Vaultaire and Obscura), 6 don't mention encryption at all, and 9 report data used to track you. Noxium, which we make, names AES-256-GCM and collects no data.

What matters in a photo vault

A photo vault is only as good as three things: how it encrypts your files, whether your photos survive a lost or new phone, and what happens if you forget your code or stop paying. Most low ratings for vault apps are about those three things, not about missing features.

  1. Real encryption, on your device. A PIN screen in front of normal files is a lock on the front door with the back door open. Look for a named cipher such as AES-256, and for encryption that happens on the iPhone before anything is uploaded.
  2. A backup that restores. Ask where the backup lives (your iCloud or the developer's servers), who holds the keys, and how you restore on a new iPhone. Backup and new-phone failures are among the most common complaints in vault app reviews.
  3. A way back in. If nobody can reset your code, you need a recovery phrase or key you keep yourself. If the developer can reset it by email, they can also, in principle, get in.
  4. Getting photos out. Check that export keeps full quality and original dates, and that it isn't behind a paywall.
  5. Honest pricing and privacy. Check whether ads or a lapsed subscription can lock your photos, and read the App Store privacy label.

Which photo vault for iPhone should you use?

It depends on what you're protecting against. Here is what the listings support, by need.

Free and built in: Apple's Hidden album

If you mainly want photos out of your main library and away from someone scrolling your phone, the Hidden album is free and locked with Face ID by default. It isn't a separate vault: anyone who knows your iPhone passcode can open it, and it syncs to your other devices. Turn on Advanced Data Protection if you want your iCloud Photos end-to-end encrypted. Read more: Is the iPhone Hidden album actually private? For a point-by-point comparison, see Hidden album vs a photo vault app.

An encrypted vault with a decoy and an iCloud backup: Noxium or Vaultaire

Both open with a drawn pattern, both say they encrypt files with AES-256-GCM, both include an encrypted iCloud backup on the free plan, and both have a decoy vault and no account. Vaultaire's free plan holds 100 photos and 10 videos. Noxium's holds 150 photos and 10 videos, adds a recovery phrase you write down at setup, and its privacy label is Data Not Collected. Comparing Vaultaire with Keepsafe instead? See Vaultaire vs Keepsafe. For the finer differences, read our Vaultaire review.

Open-source encryption code: Obscura, Encamera or Ente

Obscura says its encryption code is open source and uses AES-256-GCM, with no accounts and no cloud. Encamera says its code is open source and can store in your own iCloud. Ente Photos is open source and third-party audited, but it's an end-to-end encrypted cloud photo library with an account, not a hidden vault.

The most-reviewed: Private Photo Vault and Keepsafe

With over a million and 385,000 US ratings, these are the most familiar names. Neither listing names a cipher, both use their own cloud for backup, and both report usage data used to track you. Leaving Keepsafe? See Keepsafe alternatives with no account. Our full reviews of Private Photo Vault, Keepsafe and Privault compare each one with Noxium.

Our verdict on each app

One line per app, from what its own App Store listing says. Where we've written a full review, the app's name links to it, and all our reviews are in Noxium compared with other photo vaults.

  • Private Photo Vault: the most-rated vault on the App Store, with a decoy password, but its listing names no cipher, backups go to its own cloud, and it reports usage data used to track you.
  • Keepsafe: very popular, with AES-256 named in its help center and its own Private Cloud on Premium. It needs an account, and the listing names no decoy.
  • Privault: a second passcode opens a second folder, but the listing describes no backup and the app hasn't been updated since February 2025.
  • SPV: widely used, but the listing doesn't state encryption, a decoy or a backup, and it reports diagnostics, identifiers and usage data used to track you.
  • Secret Photo Album (SA): a decoy PIN and a "secure cloud backup", with no cipher named and the longest list of tracking data in the table.
  • PV – Secret Photo Album: a fake password and its own cloud on paid tiers; the listing says data is encrypted but doesn't name how.
  • Calculator#: a working calculator disguise with many extras and cloud export, but no encryption stated and ads in the free version. More in calculator vault apps.
  • Secret Photo Vault – Lock Photos: decoy users, a one-time Pro and Data Not Collected, but no encryption named, no backup described and no update since April 2025.
  • Safe Lock: a fake password opens a fake gallery and the label is Data Not Collected; the listing doesn't state encryption.
  • Hide it Pro: a simple hider with a fake crash screen and paid cloud storage; no encryption stated.
  • KYMS: a fake PIN opens a decoy vault and the listing says "AES military-grade", but describes no backup and the last update was June 2025.
  • MaxVault: backs up to iCloud Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive; the listing names no encryption and no decoy.
  • HiddenVault: says "zero-knowledge" without naming a cipher, and describes no backup or decoy.
  • Ente Photos: open source and third-party audited, end to end encrypted, but it's a cloud photo library with an account, not a hidden vault.
  • Encamera: open-source code and optional storage in your own iCloud; the listing names no decoy.
  • Vaultaire: AES-256-GCM, a pattern lock, a decoy with a duress vault in Pro, and a free encrypted iCloud backup. The closest app to Noxium.
  • Obscura: AES-256-GCM with open-source encryption code and no cloud at all; you back up through iOS or an encrypted export file.
  • Apple's Hidden album: free and built in, locked with Face ID, but anyone with your passcode can open it.
