Did someone open my Hidden album? How to tell, and what to do

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 23 November 2026

Short answer

iOS doesn't keep a record of who opened the Hidden album, so there's no log to check. You can look for indirect signs: Photos usage in Screen Time, missing or unhidden photos, Recently Deleted, sign-in emails from Apple, and unfamiliar devices on your Apple Account. Then change your passcode, expire the old one, and run Safety Check.

If you think someone has looked at your hidden photos, the first thing to know is what your iPhone can and can't tell you. This guide lists every place worth checking on iOS 26, what each one does and doesn't prove, and how to lock things down afterwards. For how the album works in the first place, see is the iPhone Hidden album private?

Does the iPhone record who opens the Hidden album?

No. Photos doesn't log when the Hidden album is opened, and iOS doesn't record failed Face ID attempts in a list you can see. There's also no setting that takes a photo of whoever opens it.

So any check is indirect. It can make it more or less likely that someone looked, but it can't prove it either way.

What signs can you check?

Five places can show that someone used Photos or your account when you didn't.

  1. Screen Time. Open Settings › Screen Time › See All App & Website Activity, pick the day, and look for Photos use at times you weren't using it. Screen Time has to have been on already; it doesn't record anything retroactively.
  2. The Hidden album itself. Count the photos and look for any that are missing, or that now appear in your main library because they were unhidden.
  3. Recently Deleted. In Photos, open Collections › Utilities › Recently Deleted. Deleted hidden photos stay there for up to 30 days.
  4. Apple's sign-in emails. Apple emails you when your Apple Account is used to sign in to iCloud.com in a web browser. Search your inbox for messages from Apple about a new sign-in you didn't make.
  5. Your devices. In Settings › [your name], scroll to the list of devices. Any device there with iCloud Photos on can show your hidden photos.

Sharing leaves fewer traces. If a photo was sent by AirDrop or saved to another app, there's usually no record on your iPhone. Check your Sent messages and the Recents in any app that could have been used.

Who could have opened it?

Anyone who knows your iPhone passcode, or can unlock a Mac or iPad on your account. Face ID on the Hidden album falls back to the passcode, and there's no separate password.

If you've ever unlocked your phone in front of someone, or shared your passcode, assume they could know it.

What should you do if you think someone looked?

Change your passcode, end the old one straight away, and check your account. In order:

  1. Change your iPhone passcode in Settings › Face ID & Passcode › Change Passcode. Choose Passcode Options › Custom Alphanumeric Code for a stronger one.
  2. Expire the old passcode. Since iOS 17, your previous passcode still works for 72 hours. On the same screen, tap Expire Previous Passcode Now (Apple: Expire an old passcode sooner).
  3. Check Face ID. If someone else's face was added as an alternate appearance, tap Reset Face ID and set it up again.
  4. Run Safety Check. Open Settings › Privacy & Security › Safety Check and choose Manage Sharing & Access to review and remove devices on your Apple Account (Apple: Safety Check for iPhone).
  5. Change your Apple Account password in Settings › [your name] › Sign-In & Security if anyone else might know it.
  6. Lock your Mac and iPad with codes only you know, or turn off iCloud Photos on devices others use.
Watch out

If you're worried about someone who controls your phone or could hurt you, changing settings can be noticed. Apple's Personal Safety User Guide explains how to review access safely, and Safety Check's Emergency Reset stops all sharing at once.

Questions people ask

Can I see who opened my Hidden album?

No. iOS doesn't log who opens the Hidden album or record failed Face ID attempts you can review. You can only check indirect signs, like Screen Time and missing photos.

Does the Hidden album take a photo of intruders?

No. iOS has no break-in photo feature. Some vault apps do, but they can only record attempts inside their own app.

Will changing my passcode lock someone out right away?

Not on its own. The old passcode keeps working for 72 hours unless you tap Expire Previous Passcode Now in Settings › Face ID & Passcode.

Does Screen Time show if someone opened Photos?

It shows when Photos was used and for how long, if Screen Time was already on. It doesn't show which album was opened.

Can someone see my hidden photos from their own phone?

Only if they're signed in to your Apple Account, or you hid photos inside an iCloud Shared Photo Library you share with them. Review signed-in devices with Safety Check.
