Forgot your photo vault password or PIN? What you can still do

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Don't delete or reinstall the app: that erases the vault. Then use the recovery your app was built with. Keepsafe, Private Photo Vault, PV, Calculator# and Gallery Vault reset through an email you set up; Vaultaire, Obscura and Noxium use a recovery phrase or key you saved; without either, nobody can open the vault without the code. Check the lock screen for a Forgot option, then your email and password manager, then contact the developer.

If you forgot your photo vault password, use the recovery your app was built with: an email reset, a recovery phrase or key you saved at setup, or in some apps nothing at all. Don't delete or reinstall the app while you try, because that erases the vault. This guide is for iPhone users locked out of a vault app, with steps for Private Photo Vault, Keepsafe, Gallery Vault and others, and short notes for Android vaults.

Getting locked out was one of the most common complaints when we read recent one- and two-star App Store reviews of eight vault apps in October 2026 (more on our about page). What you can do depends almost entirely on how your app was built, so start with what's safe to try.

What should you not do when you forget a vault password?

  • Don't delete or reinstall the app. Apple says deleting an app "removes the app and its related data" (Apple Support), and the vault is that data. See does deleting a vault app delete your photos?
  • Don't guess wildly. Try a few likely codes, note what you tried, then stop and look for the app's own recovery.
  • Don't pay an unlock service. No outside tool can open a properly encrypted vault without its key. If the vault isn't encrypted, the developer may be able to help you directly.
  • Don't restore an old iPhone backup over your phone hoping for an older code. You could lose newer photos and data.

What should you try first?

Work through these in order, and stop as soon as one works.

  1. Look for a "Forgot" option on the app's lock screen: forgot PIN, forgot password, use recovery phrase, or similar. Some apps show it only after wrong tries.
  2. Search your email for messages from the app: a recovery email, a setup confirmation, or a code. Search for the app's name and its developer's name.
  3. Check where you keep passwords: your password manager, your notes, or a paper note from setup.
  4. Check other devices. If the vault is also on your iPad, it might still be unlocked there.
  5. Try Face ID or Touch ID if you turned it on in the app.
  6. Contact the developer (App Store page › App Support). Give your app version and iPhone model. Never send them other passwords.

How do you reset the password in each vault app?

Use your app's own method. This is from each maker's help pages or App Store listing, checked 28 November 2026; menus can change between versions. Full reviews are linked where we've written one.

Private Photo Vault

Recovery works only if you set a recovery email when you first used the app. Its FAQ gives these steps (Private Photo Vault FAQ):

  1. App password: enter a wrong password eight times in a row, then choose Yes when the recovery prompt appears.
  2. Album password: enter a wrong album password five times in a row, then choose Yes.
  3. Cloud Vault password: this one is separate, and the company says it can't recover it. Losing it means losing access to your Cloud Vault data.

The FAQ also warns not to uninstall the app to reset a password. More in our Private Photo Vault review.

Keepsafe

Keepsafe resets your PIN with a code sent to a verified email on your account (Keepsafe Help Center):

  1. Open Keepsafe and tap Forgot PIN in the top right corner.
  2. Find the access code in your email. Keepsafe says the email is delayed by 5 minutes.
  3. Enter the code in Keepsafe, tap Submit, and create a new PIN.

If you've lost access to that email for good, Keepsafe says the account can't be recovered (Keepsafe Help Center). More in our Keepsafe review.

Other iPhone vault apps

  • PV (Secret Photo Album): set a recovery email in App Setting › Set Recovery Email; it's used if you forget the passcode. Set it before you need it (App Store).
  • Calculator#: the listing says you can retrieve a lost password through your registered email. Anyone with access to that email may be able to use it too (App Store).
  • Privault: its version 4.0 notes (April 2022) list "passcode reset with email" (App Store).
  • Hide it Pro: its US App Store page lists a "Password Reset" in-app purchase for $4.99 (checked 15 November 2026), without saying how it works. Ask the developer first. See our Hide it Pro review.
  • Secret Photo Vault - Lock Photos: an optional question and answer for password resetting, if you added one (App Store).
  • Vaultaire: a nine-word secret phrase made when you create the vault. Choose the recovery option on the unlock screen and enter the words in order, then set a new pattern. Vaultaire says support can't replace a lost pattern or phrase (Vaultaire: secret phrase).
  • Obscura: every vault has a 24-word recovery key that lets you set a new password, and the developer keeps no copy of either (App Store).
  • Noxium (which we make): you write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault. Nobody can reset a pattern: not us, not Apple. If you lose both the pattern and the phrase, nobody can open the vault. How the recovery phrase works.

Which kind of vault recovery is safest?

The one only you can use. Each kind trades convenience against who else could get in.

Ways vault apps let you back in after a forgotten code (from the makers' pages above, checked 28 November 2026)
MethodWho else could use itExamples

If an email can reset your code, your photos are only as private as that email account. A recovery phrase avoids that, but only if you write it down and keep it somewhere safe. For how a phrase opens an encrypted vault, see how encrypted photo vaults work. A security question is the weakest of these, because answers like a pet's name are often easy to find.

What if your vault is on Android, like Gallery Vault?

The Android vaults people search for most reset through an email or an account. This site is about iPhone, so here are only the makers' own steps.

  • Gallery Vault: its Google Play listing says to search your mailbox for "thinkyeah" and follow the latest mail from them. If there's none, fail to unlock twice, tap the Forgot button, then Resend Auth Email (Google Play). ThinkYeah's help center describes the same flow: tap Forgot? on the password page, verify the code, and reset (ThinkYeah support).
  • Keepsafe on Android: tap the three-dot menu, then Reset PIN and Send code, and enter the code from your email (Keepsafe Help Center).
  • Samsung Secure Folder: choose Forgot PIN, sign in with the Samsung account linked to Secure Folder, and set a new lock (Samsung Support).
  • A Tecno Spark or another Android phone: if you installed Gallery Vault from Google Play, its steps above are the same on any brand. If the lock is built into the phone's own gallery, the reset comes from the phone maker, so check its support pages.

On iPhone, several unrelated apps are called Gallery Vault. Check the maker's name under the app's name on its App Store page, and use that maker's help pages.

What if you forgot your iPhone passcode (Hidden album)?

Since iOS 16, Apple's Hidden album is locked by default and opens with Face ID or Touch ID (Apple: Hidden album). If Face ID still works, it still opens. If you changed your iPhone passcode in the last 72 hours, iOS 17 and later let you tap Forgot Passcode? on the lock screen, then Enter Previous Passcode. The phone needs to be online, signed in to your Apple Account, with Find My on (Apple: use your old passcode).

Otherwise, the way back is to erase the iPhone with a computer, which Apple says erases the data on it (Apple: reset your iPhone and passcode). Anything that was only on the phone, and not in iCloud or a backup, is lost.

How do you avoid getting locked out next time?

  • Save the code in a password manager, with a note of which app it's for.
  • Set up recovery now, whatever your app offers, and test it once.
  • Write a recovery phrase on paper and keep it somewhere safe, away from the phone.
  • Keep a backup you understand, so a lockout isn't also a loss. See photo vault recovery for lost photos.
  • Write down every code you use. A decoy vault means a second code to remember, and it's the one people forget.

Questions people ask

How do I reset a forgotten vault password?

Use the app's Forgot option on its lock screen. Most vaults then send a code to the recovery email you set up; others ask for a recovery phrase or key you saved. If you set up neither, contact the developer before you try anything else.

How do I unlock Private Photo Vault if I forgot the password?

Enter a wrong password eight times in a row and choose Yes at the recovery prompt. This works only if you set a recovery email when you first used the app. A forgotten Cloud Vault password can't be recovered.

How can I find out my vault password?

Usually only from where you saved it: a password manager, your notes, or your email. If you can't find it, use the app's reset or recovery option instead.

What if I forgot my Gallery Vault password?

On Android, search your email for "thinkyeah" and follow the latest reset mail. If there isn't one, fail to unlock twice, tap Forgot and then Resend Auth Email. On iPhone, check which company makes your Gallery Vault and follow its help pages.

How do I recover a forgotten vault password on Android, for example on a Tecno Spark?

Use the vault app's own reset, which is the same on any phone brand. For a lock built into the phone's gallery, follow the phone maker's support pages. Don't clear the app's data or uninstall it, because that can delete the hidden files.

Is there software that can unlock a photo vault?

Not for a properly encrypted vault. Treat any paid unlock tool or service with suspicion, and contact the app's developer instead.
