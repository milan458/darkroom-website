Guide · Locking photos

How to lock photos on iPhone with Face ID, a passcode or a pattern

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Hide the photos: touch and hold a photo in Photos and tap Hide. Since iOS 16, the Hidden album is locked with Face ID by default; check that Settings › Apps › Photos › Use Face ID is on. You can also make the whole Photos app require Face ID, or put photos in a locked note. The Hidden album and the app lock also open with your iPhone passcode. For a lock that's separate from your passcode, use a locked note with its own password or a vault app.

To lock photos on iPhone with Face ID, hide them: in Photos, touch and hold a photo and tap Hide. Hidden photos go to the Hidden album, which has been locked with Face ID by default since iOS 16. This guide is for anyone who wants to put Face ID on their photo gallery, keep a few photos away from people who borrow their phone, or find a lock that's separate from their iPhone passcode.

There's no "lock this album" button in Photos. Instead, iPhone has locks at three levels: the Hidden album, the whole Photos app, and a single note. Each one is below, with what it actually stops and what it doesn't.

How do you put Face ID on photos in the Photos app?

Hide the photos, then check that the Face ID switch for Photos is on. Apple's menu paths, checked 28 November 2026:

  1. Hide one photo: in Photos, touch and hold the photo, tap Hide, then confirm (Apple Support).
  2. Hide several: tap Select, tap the photos, tap the More (…) button, then Hide (Apple Personal Safety User Guide).
  3. Check the lock: open Settings › Apps › Photos and make sure Use Face ID is on. It's on by default in iOS 16 and later, and it locks both the Hidden and the Recently Deleted albums. If you turn it off, Apple says the Hidden album is unlocked too (Apple Support).
  4. Test it: in Photos, tap Collections, scroll to Utilities › Hidden, and tap View Album. It should ask for Face ID before showing anything.
  5. Optional: in Settings › Apps › Photos, turn off Show Hidden Album so the album doesn't appear under Utilities at all.

What it doesn't protect: Apple's Personal Safety guide says the Hidden album needs "Face ID, Touch ID, or a passcode" to unlock, so someone who knows your iPhone passcode can open it. With iCloud Photos on, photos you hide on one device are hidden on your other devices too (Apple Support), so check the lock on each one. How do you lock the whole Photos app with Face ID?

Touch and hold the Photos icon and tap Require Face ID. Then Photos asks for Face ID every time it opens. Apple's steps (Apple Personal Safety User Guide):

  1. Touch and hold the Photos icon on the Home Screen until the quick actions menu opens.
  2. Tap Require Face ID (or Touch ID or Passcode).
  3. Tap Require Face ID again and authenticate.

This stops someone you've handed your unlocked phone from browsing your whole library. Apple says information from a locked app may also stay out of notification previews, search and Siri suggestions. What it doesn't protect: the passcode still opens the app, the lock applies only to that one device and doesn't sync through iCloud, and it's all or nothing, so you'll unlock your whole library many times a day.

Showing someone one photo: Guided Access

To hand over your phone with one photo on screen, use Guided Access. It keeps the phone in the app you're in (Apple Support).

  1. Turn it on once in Settings › Accessibility › Guided Access and set a Guided Access passcode.
  2. Open the photo, triple-click the side button, and tap Start. To stop swipes to other photos, circle the screen to disable it, or turn off Touch in the session settings.
  3. To end it, triple-click the side button, authenticate, and tap End.

How do you lock photos with a separate password?

Put the photos in a note and lock it with a Notes password. It's the only built-in lock that can be separate from your iPhone passcode. Apple's steps (Apple Support):

  1. Set a Notes password: open Settings › Apps › Notes › Password, create a password only for Notes, and turn on Face ID if you want it as a shortcut. Since iOS 16, the other choice is to lock notes with your iPhone passcode.
  2. Add the photos to a note in Notes.
  3. Lock the note: open it, tap the More (…) button, then Lock.
  4. Delete the originals from Photos, then from Recently Deleted.

What it doesn't protect: Apple says notes with video, audio, PDFs or tags can't be locked, so this suits a handful of photos, not videos or a library. There are no albums. And if you forget a separate Notes password, resetting it doesn't give you access to the notes you already locked. Can you lock one album, or use a pattern instead of Face ID?

Not in Photos. Only the Hidden and Recently Deleted albums have a lock, and iPhone's own locks use Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode. To lock an album of your choice, or to use a pattern, you need a vault app.

A lock is only as good as what's behind it. In some vault apps the code just opens a screen in front of ordinary files; in others it's used to make the encryption key. Ask how the app uses it before you trust it. We explain the difference in how encrypted photo vaults work.

Questions people ask

How do I put Face ID on my gallery on iPhone?

Hide the photos and check that Settings › Apps › Photos › Use Face ID is on: the Hidden album then asks for Face ID. To put Face ID on the whole gallery, touch and hold the Photos icon and tap Require Face ID.

Can someone open my Hidden album without Face ID?

Yes, if they know your iPhone passcode, because the lock accepts a passcode. If Use Face ID is off in Photos settings, the album doesn't ask at all.

Can I lock a specific album on iPhone?

No. Photos only locks the Hidden and Recently Deleted albums. To lock other photos, hide them, put them in a locked note, or use a vault app with album locks.

Can I lock photos with a different password than my iPhone passcode?

Yes, in two ways: a locked note with a separate Notes password, or a vault app with its own PIN, password or pattern.

Does locking the Photos app hide it?

No. Require Face ID only asks for Face ID when Photos opens. The app stays on your Home Screen.

Does hiding a photo encrypt it?

Not with a separate key. Hiding moves the photo to the Hidden album and puts Face ID or your passcode in front of it; Apple's Hidden album page describes no extra encryption. Vault apps that derive a key from your code work differently.
