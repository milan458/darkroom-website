How to hide photos on iPhone: every method, and what each one protects

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

To hide a photo on iPhone, open Photos, touch and hold the photo, tap Hide and confirm. It moves to the Hidden album, which needs Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode to open. This guide is for anyone who wants photos out of sight on their iPhone, and wants to know which method also keeps them from people who know the passcode.

There are four ways to do it, and they protect against different people. Hiding a photo from someone scrolling your camera roll is easy. Keeping it from someone who knows your passcode, or who picks up your iPad, takes more. Each section below gives the exact steps from Apple Support and says what the method doesn't protect.

Which way of hiding photos should you use?

Use the Hidden album to keep photos out of sight. Use a vault app with its own code when the photos must stay private even from people who know your passcode.

How do you hide photos with the Hidden album?

Touch and hold a photo, tap Hide, and confirm. The photo leaves your library and appears only in the Hidden album (Apple: Hide photos with the Hidden album). It isn't deleted: Apple's Personal Safety guide says hidden items "remain in your library".

  1. Open Photos and find the photo or video.
  2. Touch and hold it, then tap Hide and confirm.
  3. To hide several at once, tap Select, tap each photo, then tap the More (…) button and choose Hide.

To see hidden photos, tap Collections, scroll down to Utilities, tap Hidden, then View Album, and unlock with Face ID or Touch ID. To unhide one, touch and hold it and tap Unhide. If Hidden isn't there, or you want to know where hidden photos go on iCloud or a new iPhone, see the complete guide to hidden photos on iPhone.

To take the Hidden album itself out of view, go to Settings › Apps › Photos and turn off Show Hidden Album. Apple's Personal Safety guide places this switch under Albums on that screen, so look there if you don't see it straight away. Turning it off hides the album; it doesn't lock anything.

What the Hidden album doesn't protect:

  • Your passcode opens it. The lock accepts Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode, so anyone who knows the passcode can see your hidden photos.
  • It syncs. With iCloud Photos on, a photo you hide on your iPhone is hidden, but present, on your iPad and Mac too.
  • iCloud holds the keys by default. Photos in iCloud are end-to-end encrypted only if you turn on Advanced Data Protection (Apple: iCloud data security overview).

How do you put Face ID on the Photos app?

The Hidden album already has Face ID on it. To put Face ID on the whole Photos app, the "gallery" in many people's searches, lock the app from its icon. Apple's guide describes this for iOS 18 and later.

  1. Touch and hold the Photos icon on your Home Screen until the quick actions menu opens.
  2. Tap Require Face ID (or Touch ID, or Passcode).
  3. Tap it again to confirm, then authenticate with Face ID.

These steps are from Apple's Personal Safety guide: lock or hide an app. The Hidden album's own lock lives in Photos settings, under Settings › Apps › Photos. Leave it on.

What locking the app doesn't do: it locks your whole library rather than single photos, your passcode still opens it, and the lock applies only to the device where you set it. Apple says a locked app's information stays out of places such as notification previews, search and Siri suggestions. Only apps you download can be hidden, so the Photos icon stays on your Home Screen.

How do you hide photos on iPad and Mac?

On iPad it works as on iPhone: touch and hold the photo and tap Hide. To find the Hidden album, you may need to tap the sidebar button in the upper-left corner first. On a Mac, Control-click the photo and choose Hide Photo, or press Command-L, then confirm. With iCloud Photos on, a photo you hide on one device is hidden on all of them (Apple).

On a Mac the Hidden album is off by default. To see it, choose View › Show Hidden Photo Album in Photos, then unlock it with Touch ID or your login password.

How do you hide a photo in a locked note?

Add the photo to a note, then lock the note. A locked note can use a password separate from your passcode, which makes it stronger than the Hidden album for a handful of photos.

  1. Open Notes and start a new note stored on your iPhone or in iCloud.
  2. Add the photos with the camera button.
  3. Tap the More (…) button and choose Lock, then pick Use iPhone Passcode or Create Password. A separate password lives in Settings › Apps › Notes › Password.
  4. Delete the originals from Photos, then from Recently Deleted (see below).

The limits, from Apple: Lock notes on iPhone: you can't lock a note that contains video, audio, PDFs or tags, or a note you share. If you forget a separate Notes password, Apple can't help you open those notes. Notes also have no albums or grid view.

When should you use a photo vault app?

Use a vault app when photos must stay private from people who know your passcode, or when you have more than a handful. A good vault has its own code, encrypts files on the iPhone, and backs them up in a way you can restore.

Many vault apps only put a PIN screen in front of ordinary files. Before you trust one with photos you can't replace, check four things:

  • Does it name its encryption? Look for something specific, such as AES-256, done on the device. How encrypted photo vaults work explains what the terms mean.
  • Where does the backup go, and does it restore on a new iPhone?
  • What if you forget your code? You want a recovery option you control, not an email reset the developer can use too.
  • Does it have a decoy? If someone might make you open the app, a second code that opens a harmless vault helps. See what a decoy vault is.

Which mistakes leave hidden photos visible?

Most hidden photos that get seen weren't found in the Hidden album. A copy stayed somewhere else, or the lock was off. Check these:

  1. Treating Show Hidden Album as a lock. Turning it off only takes the album out of view. Anyone holding your unlocked iPhone can turn it back on in Settings.
  2. Hiding photos in a Shared Library. In the iCloud Shared Photo Library, a hidden photo moves to the Hidden album for every participant, and any of them can view or unhide it (Apple: Hide photos on iCloud.com).
  3. Leaving it in Recently Deleted. Deleted photos can be recovered there for 30 days (Apple: If you're missing photos). Open Collections › Utilities › Recently Deleted, unlock it, and delete them for good.
  4. Forgetting other copies. A photo you sent or received in Messages, WhatsApp or email has its own copy there. So do old imports on a Mac or PC.
  5. Erasing the iPhone without a backup. Hidden photos are part of your library. If iCloud Photos is off and there's no recent backup, erasing the iPhone deletes them with everything else.

Questions people ask

How can I hide photos on my iPhone?

Open Photos, touch and hold the photo, tap Hide and confirm. For several, tap Select, choose them, tap More (…) and Hide. They move to the Hidden album in Collections › Utilities.

How do I put Face ID on my gallery on iPhone?

Touch and hold the Photos icon, tap Require Face ID, tap it again and authenticate. The Hidden album inside Photos is already locked by default. Your passcode still opens both.

Does hiding a photo on iPhone delete it?

No. The photo stays in your library and in iCloud Photos, shown only in the Hidden album. You can unhide it at any time.

Can someone see my hidden photos without my Face ID?

Yes, if they know your iPhone passcode, or can unlock another device signed in to your Apple Account with iCloud Photos on.

How do I hide videos on iPhone?

The same way: touch and hold the video in Photos and tap Hide. A locked note can't hold video, so use the Hidden album or a vault app. See how to hide videos on iPhone.

Can I hide the Hidden album?

Yes. In Settings › Apps › Photos, turn off Show Hidden Album. The album disappears from the Photos app until you turn it back on. It's out of view, not locked.
