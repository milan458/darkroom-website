Is the iPhone Hidden album actually private?

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Yes from casual eyes, no from anyone who knows your passcode. The Hidden album is locked by default on iOS 16 and later and opens with Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone passcode. It isn't a separate vault: the passcode opens it, it syncs to your other devices with iCloud Photos, and in iCloud it's end-to-end encrypted only if you turn on Advanced Data Protection.

Yes, the iPhone Hidden album is safe from someone scrolling through your unlocked phone. No, it isn't safe from anyone who knows your iPhone passcode, can unlock your iPad or Mac, or can make you unlock it. This guide is for people who searched "is the iPhone Hidden album safe" and want to know exactly who it keeps out, and who it doesn't.

The Hidden album is the first place most people put private photos, because it's free and already on the iPhone. It's good at one job: keeping photos out of your main library. Here is what it protects, what it doesn't, and how to make it stricter, from Apple's own documentation.

What does the Hidden album actually do?

It takes photos out of view. A hidden photo shows up only in the Hidden album, under Collections › Utilities (Apple: If you're missing photos).

On iOS 16, iPadOS 16.1 and macOS Ventura and later, the Hidden album is locked by default and needs authentication to open in Photos (Apple: Hide photos with the Hidden album). You can also take the album out of view with Settings › Apps › Photos › Show Hidden Album. For step-by-step help finding, viewing and adding to it, see how to view hidden photos on iPhone.

What it doesn't do is move, copy or re-encrypt the photo. Apple's Personal Safety guide says hidden photos "remain in your library", and Apple's developer documentation shows that "hidden" is a property of the photo (Apple: includeHiddenAssets). Apple doesn't describe any extra encryption for hidden photos.

Who can see your hidden photos?

Anyone who can unlock your iPhone with its passcode, or unlock another device signed in to your Apple Account, can see them. Here's how it plays out with default settings.

Apps you give full access to your library are a grey area. Apple's developer documentation lets an app ask for hidden photos along with the rest. It doesn't say whether the Face ID lock stops that, so we don't claim it does.

Why are your passcode and your other devices the weak points?

Because the Face ID lock on the Hidden album isn't a separate lock. If Face ID fails or is skipped, your iPhone passcode opens the album. Partners, children and housemates often know that passcode, or have watched it typed. A six-digit code, the default length (Apple: Use a passcode), is easy to shoulder-surf in public.

The second weak point is syncing. With iCloud Photos on, photos you hide on one device are hidden on your other devices too (Apple). They exist on every iPad and Mac signed in to your Apple Account. On a Mac, the Hidden album is off by default and opens with Touch ID or the login password. On a shared family iPad, anyone who can unlock it can open the album. If you think someone already has, see did someone open my Hidden album?

The third is the Shared Library. In the iCloud Shared Photo Library, a hidden photo moves to the Hidden album for every participant, "where they can view or stop hiding it" (Apple).

Is the Hidden album encrypted in iCloud?

End-to-end, only with Advanced Data Protection. With standard data protection, iCloud Photos is encrypted in transit and on Apple's servers, but Apple holds the keys. With Advanced Data Protection on, Photos is end-to-end encrypted and the keys stay on your trusted devices (Apple: iCloud data security overview).

To turn it on, go to Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Advanced Data Protection and tap Turn On. You'll need two-factor authentication, iOS 16.2 or later on your iPhone, and a recovery contact or recovery key, because Apple no longer has the keys to help you recover that data (Apple: Turn on Advanced Data Protection).

How do you make the Hidden album more private?

  1. Keep the lock on. It's on by default; the setting lives in Settings › Apps › Photos. Don't turn it off.
  2. Hide the album. Turn off Show Hidden Album on the same screen. This takes it out of view; it doesn't lock it.
  3. Use a stronger passcode. In Settings › Face ID & Passcode › Change Passcode › Passcode Options, choose a custom alphanumeric code (Apple).
  4. Turn on Advanced Data Protection for end-to-end encrypted iCloud Photos.
  5. Check where else the photos live: Recently Deleted (30 days), shared albums, the Shared Library, Messages, and any Mac or iPad on your account.

When is the Hidden album not enough?

When the people you're worried about know your passcode, share your devices, or could make you unlock your phone. Questions people ask

Is the iPhone Hidden album safe?

Safe from casual browsing, not from anyone who knows your passcode. It opens with Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode, and syncs to your other devices with iCloud Photos.

Can someone see my Hidden album without Face ID?

Yes, if they know your iPhone passcode. The lock accepts Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode.

Does the Hidden album show up on my Mac and iPad?

Yes, if iCloud Photos is on. On a Mac, the Hidden album is off by default and opens with Touch ID or your login password.

Is the Hidden album safe from Apple?

Only with Advanced Data Protection on. With standard protection, Apple holds the keys for iCloud Photos. With Advanced Data Protection, Photos is end-to-end encrypted.

Do hidden photos get deleted if I turn off Show Hidden Album?

No. Turning it off only takes the album out of view. The photos stay in your library until you unhide or delete them.
