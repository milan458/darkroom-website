Can a photo vault block screenshots on iPhone?

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 25 November 2026

Short answer

No. iOS gives apps no way to stop a screenshot. An app is told only after one is taken, when the image is already in Photos. What a vault can do is hide its content while the screen is recorded or mirrored, and in the app switcher. Nothing stops someone photographing the screen with another phone.

"Screenshot protection" sounds like a wall, but on iPhone it's closer to an alarm. Some vault apps advertise it, and it's easy to assume they stop screenshots. This guide explains what iOS lets apps do, the kinds of protection vault apps offer, what no app can stop, and how to test the vault you use. For the bigger picture, see are photo vault apps safe?

What does iOS let apps do about screen capture?

Apps can notice capture, but not prevent it. Apple's developer documentation describes these signals:

What an iPhone app can and can't do about screen capture, from Apple's developer documentation
CaptureCan the app block it?What the app can do
ScreenshotNoLearn afterwards that one was taken; the image is already saved
Screen recordingNoNotice that the screen is being captured, and hide its content
AirPlay or mirroringNoSame signal as recording: hide content while it lasts
App switcher previewYes, for its own previewCover its screen before iOS takes the preview
Another phone's cameraNoNothing

Apple's own article on protecting sensitive content when screen sharing says the capture signal doesn't tell recording, mirroring and remote control apart. An app just knows the screen is being captured, and can blank out what's private until it stops.

What kinds of screenshot protection do vault apps offer?

Usually one or more of these four:

  • A screenshot alert. The app notices a screenshot and warns you or logs it. The screenshot still exists, in Photos.
  • Hiding during recording and mirroring. The vault turns black or shows a cover while the screen is captured. This is the most useful of the four, though the first moment of a recording can catch what was on screen before the app reacts.
  • A blank app switcher card. When you leave the app, it covers its screen, so the switcher doesn't show your last photo.
  • Blank-in-screenshot tricks. A few apps draw content in a way that comes out blank in screenshots. This isn't an Apple feature for that purpose, and it can stop working after an iOS update.

Read a vault's claims closely. "Screenshot detection" means an alert after the fact, not a block.

What can't any app stop?

Anything outside the phone, and anything you've already taken out of the vault:

  • Another camera. Someone can photograph or film your screen with a second phone.
  • A screenshot taken anyway. An alert doesn't delete it. It sits in Photos and uploads to iCloud Photos if that's on.
  • Exported copies. Photos you saved back to Photos or sent to someone are out of the vault's reach.
  • Someone with the vault open. If a person can unlock it, they can see everything in it.
  • Spyware or a managed phone. An iPhone run by an employer or school can be configured by someone else. See is the iPhone Hidden album private? for what others with access can see.

What if someone took a screenshot of your vault?

Find it and delete it, then empty Recently Deleted. Screenshots go into your Photos library like any other photo.

  1. Open Photos and look in the Screenshots collection under Media Types, or at the newest items in your library.
  2. Delete the screenshot, and confirm.
  3. Empty it from Recently Deleted under Utilities, or iOS keeps it for 30 days.
  4. Check your other devices and iCloud.com if iCloud Photos is on. Deleting on one device deletes it everywhere, but check it has synced.
  5. Change your vault's code or pattern if someone else took it, and lock the vault whenever you hand over your phone.

How do you test the vault app you use?

Try each kind of capture yourself, with a harmless test photo in the vault:

  1. Screenshot. Open the photo in the vault and press the side button and volume up together. Open Photos and look at the screenshot: is the photo visible or blank? Did the vault warn you?
  2. Screen recording. Start a recording from Control Center, open the vault and the photo, then stop. Watch the recording in Photos.
  3. App switcher. With the photo open, swipe up from the bottom and pause. Look at the vault's card.
  4. Clean up. Delete the test screenshot and recording, and empty Recently Deleted.

Questions people ask

Can an iPhone app detect a screenshot?

Yes, but only after it's taken. The screenshot is already saved in Photos by the time the app finds out.

Can a photo vault block screen recording?

It can't stop the recording, but it can notice it and hide its content, so the recording shows a blank or covered screen.

Does the app switcher show my vault photos?

Only if the vault doesn't cover its screen. Test it: open a photo, swipe up and pause, and look at the card.

Can someone photograph my vault with another phone?

Yes. No app can prevent that. Lock the vault before you hand your phone to anyone.
