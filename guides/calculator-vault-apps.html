Calculator vault apps: how they work and what they hide

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 16 November 2026

Short answer

A calculator vault app looks and works like a calculator, but typing a secret code and = opens a hidden store of photos and videos. The disguise stops a quick glance at your Home Screen. It doesn't stop someone who checks Settings, and many of these apps name no encryption, show ads or track you, so read the listing before you trust one.

Calculator vaults are some of the most common photo hiders on the App Store. This guide explains how the disguise works, what it protects and what it doesn't, and what three calculator vaults say about themselves on their App Store listings, checked on 16 November 2026. We make Noxium, a photo vault with an optional calculator lookalike icon, so we say where it fits and where it doesn't.

How does a calculator vault app work?

It opens as a working calculator. A code you choose, typed on the keypad and followed by =, opens the vault instead of showing a result.

  1. The icon and first screen look like a calculator. Anyone who opens it sees sums work as normal.
  2. Your code is the key. At setup you pick a number, such as a PIN. Typing it and pressing = opens the hidden part.
  3. Inside is a gallery. You import photos and videos from Photos, and many apps offer to delete the originals for you.
  4. Extras vary. Some add decoy codes, a panic switch, notes, passwords, contacts or cloud backup.

The disguise is a social tool: it makes the app look boring to someone scrolling through your phone. Whether the photos are encrypted is a separate question, and the calculator screen says nothing about it. For the difference between hiding and encrypting, see how encrypted photo vaults work.

What does the calculator disguise protect against?

It protects against a casual look. Someone swiping through your Home Screen, or opening the app, sees a calculator.

  • A partner, child or friend holding your phone for a minute is unlikely to try a code on a calculator.
  • A photo popping up in the wrong place. Once you delete the originals from the Photos app, they no longer show up in Memories, widgets or Photos search.

What doesn't a calculator vault protect against?

Anyone who looks at the phone's lists of apps, or at the files themselves. The disguise lives only on the Home Screen.

  • The App Store. Your list of purchased apps shows each app by its full store name, and a name like "Calculator Lock - Secure Vault" gives the game away.
  • iPhone Storage. Settings › General › iPhone Storage lists every app and how much space it uses. A "calculator" holding gigabytes of videos stands out.
  • Someone who knows the trick. Calculator vaults are well known. A second calculator next to Apple's own is a hint.
  • A copy of the phone's storage. If the files aren't encrypted, anyone who can read the app's storage can see them. None of the three listings above names any encryption.
  • A lost phone. An app with no backup loses everything with the phone. Private Photos (Calculator%) says its data stays only on the device.
  • The app itself. Two of the three apps above carry the "Data Used to Track You" privacy label. A vault that tracks you is an odd place for private photos. See are photo vault apps safe? for what each label means.

How do you check a calculator vault before you trust it?

Read four things on its App Store page: encryption, privacy label, backup and last update. It takes two minutes.

  1. Search the description for "encrypt". Look for a named method, such as AES-256. "Secure" and "protected" alone don't say how.
  2. Scroll to App Privacy. "Data Not Collected" is best. "Data Used to Track You" means the app shares data for tracking.
  3. Find out where backups go. Your own iCloud, the developer's cloud, or nowhere. If it's the developer's cloud, look for whether it's encrypted before upload.
  4. Check Version History. An app not updated in over a year may not keep up with new iOS versions.
  5. Test before you move everything. Add a few photos, close the app, and open it again with your code. Only then delete originals from Photos, and empty Recently Deleted.

What are the alternatives to a calculator vault?

Pick by what you're hiding from. For a quick glance, iPhone's own tools may be enough. For anyone who might dig, you want encryption.

  • iPhone's Hidden album. Free and built in, locked with Face ID or your passcode. Anyone who knows your passcode can open it. See Hidden album vs a photo vault app.
  • Lock the Photos app. On iOS 18 and later, touch and hold the Photos icon and tap Require Face ID. Our guide to locking photos with Face ID has the steps.

Questions people ask

Are calculator vault apps safe?

They're safe from a quick look, not from a determined one. Check that the listing names encryption, that the privacy label isn't "Data Used to Track You", and that it was updated in the last year.

Can someone find photos hidden in a calculator app?

They can find the app: your App Store purchases list it by its full name, and iPhone Storage shows how much it holds. Seeing the photos still takes your code, or access to the files if they aren't encrypted.

Do calculator vault apps encrypt photos?

Some may, but none of the three we checked says so on its listing. If an app doesn't name an encryption method, assume the photos are only hidden.

What happens if I forget my calculator code?

It depends on the app: some use an email or security question, and some have no way back. Don't delete the app first; read our guide to a forgotten vault password.

Is there a calculator vault without ads?

Yes. Private Photos (Calculator%) lists no ads and collects no data, but it hasn't been updated since 2021. Our list of vaults without subscriptions or ads has more.
