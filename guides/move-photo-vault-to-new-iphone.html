How to move a private photo vault to a new iPhone without losing photos

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Back up the vault inside the vault app first, then restore it inside the app on the new iPhone. Quick Start and iCloud Backup bring the app across, but the photos come back only if the app's own backup has them. Save a copy of anything you can't replace, and erase the old iPhone only after every photo opens on the new one.

To move a photo vault to a new iPhone, back up the vault inside the vault app, then restore it inside the app on the new phone. Quick Start or an iCloud backup brings the app across, but the photos inside it come back only if the app's own backup has them. This guide is for anyone with photos in a vault app, such as Private Photo Vault, Keepsafe, Calculator#, Vaultaire or Noxium, who is about to switch iPhones.

Why photos go missing: an iPhone backup is not a copy of everything. Apple says iCloud backups include what's on the device that doesn't already sync to iCloud, and leave out data stored in other cloud services and Face ID settings. So a vault kept in the developer's own cloud comes back only through that app's login, a vault the app never finished backing up doesn't come back at all, and Face ID has to be set up again before it opens anything. The four steps below work for any vault app.

Step 1: Where does your vault app keep its backup?

Open the vault's settings and find its backup, sync or cloud section. Answer three questions: where does the backup go, is it on, and what will you need to restore it?

Step 2: Finish the backup and save a safety copy

Make sure the vault's backup is complete, then keep a second copy of what you can't replace. Do this on the old iPhone, before you start the new one.

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and power, and open the vault app. Look for "backed up", a last-backup time, or a count of items still waiting.
  2. Back up the iPhone too. Go to Settings › [your name] › iCloud › iCloud Backup and tap Back Up Now. The date and time of the last backup shows under Back Up Now (Apple Support). If iPhone says there isn't enough iCloud storage, the backup won't finish until you free up or add space.
  3. Write down the counts. Note how many photos and videos the vault holds, so you can compare on the new phone.
  4. Export a safety copy of your most important photos and videos to somewhere you control, such as a Mac or an encrypted external drive. Check that the export keeps full quality and dates (see how to export photos from a vault app). Exported copies are no longer protected by the vault, so keep them private and delete them once the move is done.
  5. Write down every way in: the vault code, any recovery phrase or recovery email, and the code for each extra vault, such as a decoy vault. Face ID settings aren't in an iCloud backup (Apple Support), so expect to type the code on the new phone first. If you've lost it, read forgot your photo vault password? before you switch.

Step 3: Set up the new iPhone, then restore inside the vault app

Move the iPhone with Quick Start or from an iCloud backup, signed in to the same Apple Account. Then open the vault app and restore the vault from its own backup.

With Quick Start (Apple Support):

  1. Put the new iPhone next to the old one. The old device needs iOS 11 or later, Wi-Fi on and Bluetooth on. Plug both in.
  2. Follow the setup screens on the new iPhone and choose how to move your data. Downloading from iCloud lets you use the new phone while apps and data arrive in the background. Transferring directly from the old phone means neither phone can be used until the transfer finishes.
  3. Keep both phones near each other and plugged in until the move is complete.

From an iCloud backup (Apple Support):

  1. Turn on the new iPhone and follow the setup steps until you reach Transfer Your Apps & Data.
  2. Tap From iCloud Backup and sign in to your Apple Account.
  3. Choose the most recent backup, after checking its date and size.
  4. Stay on Wi-Fi and power until the progress bar finishes. If the phone is already set up, Apple says to erase it before you follow these steps.

Then restore the vault: open the vault app and follow its own restore steps, such as logging in, entering your code or choosing restore. Large libraries can take hours, so keep the new phone on Wi-Fi and power.

Step 4: Check every photo, then erase the old iPhone

Erase the old iPhone only after the new one passes these checks. Until then, the old phone is your copy.

  1. Compare the counts with the ones you wrote down in Step 2.
  2. Open a few full-size photos and play a few videos, including old ones, not just thumbnails.
  3. Check albums, notes and every extra vault, such as a decoy.
  4. Delete your safety copies from Step 2 once everything is there.
  5. Then erase the old iPhone.

If something is missing, stop. Don't delete or reinstall the vault app on either phone, and don't erase the old iPhone. Reinstalling can remove files that might still be recoverable. Our guide to finding lost or deleted vault photos walks through what to try next.

Questions people ask

Does Quick Start move my photo vault app?

It moves the app, but the photos come back only if the vault's own backup, or the iPhone backup, has them. Check where your vault backs up (Step 1) before you switch.

Do I need the same Apple Account on the new iPhone?

Yes, for vaults that back up to your own iCloud: the backup lives in that Apple Account. For vaults that use their own cloud, you need that app's login instead.

Will Face ID open my vault on the new iPhone?

Not straight away. Apple's iCloud backups don't include Face ID settings, so set up Face ID on the new iPhone and expect to enter the vault's code, PIN or pattern first.

Can I keep my vault on both the old and the new iPhone?

Only if the app syncs between devices. Most vaults restore a backup onto one phone and don't keep two devices in step, so look for sync, not just backup, in the app's settings. Backups in your own iCloud only restore on devices signed in to the same Apple Account.

What if vault photos are already missing after the switch?

Stop and keep both phones as they are. Don't reinstall the app or erase the old iPhone. Check whether the old phone still opens the vault, look for a restore option or a web login, and email the developer with your app version and what happened.

How long does restoring a vault take?

It depends on the size of the library and your connection. Thousands of photos and long videos can take hours. Keep the new phone on Wi-Fi and power, and don't erase the old one until it's done.
