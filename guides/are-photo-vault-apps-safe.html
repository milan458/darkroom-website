Are photo vault apps safe? What to check before you trust one

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Some are. A safe photo vault encrypts photos on your iPhone with a key made from your own code, keeps an encrypted backup you can restore on a new phone, and doesn't track you. Many vaults only put a PIN screen in front of ordinary files, and of 19 vault and private photo apps we checked in November 2026, 10 report data used to track you. Check the 7 points below before you trust one.

A photo vault app is safe when it encrypts your photos on your iPhone with a key made from your own code, and lets you restore them on a new phone. Many vaults only put a PIN screen in front of ordinary files, and some track you, so "safe" depends on the app, not the category. This guide is for anyone deciding whether to trust a vault (Keepsafe or any other) with private photos, or wondering how the one they already use works.

"Is it safe?" really asks two things: can other people see my photos, and could I lose them? A good vault gets both right. Below is a 7-point checklist you can run on any vault, with Keepsafe as a worked example.

How does a photo vault app work?

A vault app keeps photos inside its own storage, away from the Photos app, and asks for a code before it shows them. What matters is what happens to the files behind that code.

  • A lock screen only. The app stores normal image files and shows a PIN screen first. The PIN guards the app's screens, not the files themselves.
  • Real encryption with your key. The app turns each photo into scrambled data that can only be read with a key made from your code. Without the code, the files are useless, even to the developer.

Your iPhone already encrypts app files with Data Protection, and third-party apps get it automatically. For the strongest class, Apple says the key "is protected with a key derived from the user passcode or password and the device UID" (Apple Platform Security). That protects a lost or locked phone. It doesn't protect against someone who knows your iPhone passcode, or who picks up your phone while it's unlocked. A vault's own encryption adds a second key that isn't your iPhone passcode. For the longer version, see how photo vault apps work.

What are the real risks of photo vault apps?

  • No real encryption. If a vault only hides files behind a PIN screen, anyone who reaches those files another way can see them.
  • Losing your photos. If a vault keeps photos only on the phone, a broken phone, a reinstall or a new iPhone can leave them stranded.
  • Getting locked out. A forgotten code with no recovery option can mean losing everything.
  • Someone else getting in. If the developer can reset your code by email, anyone who controls that email may be able to as well.
  • Your data going elsewhere. Ad and analytics tools send activity data off your phone. Backups on the developer's servers can mean your photos leave your control.
  • Paywalls around your own photos. Some apps limit access or export when you stop paying.

What should you check before trusting a photo vault?

Run these 7 checks on the App Store page, the app's help pages and the app itself. Most take a minute each.

  1. Named encryption, done on the phone. Look for a specific scheme such as AES-256-GCM, and a statement that photos are encrypted on the device. GCM is a NIST-standard mode that encrypts and also lets the app detect if a file was changed (NIST SP 800-38D calls it "authenticated encryption"). "Secure" and "military-grade" on their own tell you nothing.
  2. Where the key comes from. The safest answer is: from your code, through a slow key-derivation function, and never stored. NIST describes this approach for protecting stored data in SP 800-132. If the key sits next to the files, or the developer holds it, someone other than you can open them.
  3. Account or not. Does the app need an email account? An account isn't bad in itself, but find out what it can do. If the account can reset your code, the security of your vault now depends on your email.
  4. A backup you can restore. Where does it go, is it encrypted with your key, is it on the free plan, and how do you restore on a new iPhone? Our new-iPhone checklist covers the restore.
  5. The privacy label. On the App Store page, scroll to App Privacy. "Data Used to Track You" means data from the app is combined with other companies' data for ads or shared with data brokers. For a vault, the best answer is "Data Not Collected". The labels are self-reported by developers (Apple Support), so treat them as a claim.
  6. Recovery that doesn't weaken privacy. A recovery phrase or key that only you keep is safer than an email reset the developer controls. No recovery at all is private but unforgiving.
  7. What it doesn't protect. A good vault says so. No vault protects photos that are still in Photos or Recently Deleted, copies in iCloud Photos or chats, someone who watches you enter your code, or a moment when someone makes you open the app. For that last one, read how decoy vaults and duress codes work.

Is Keepsafe photo vault safe?

Keepsafe says it encrypts your files, and it's one of the most used vaults, but it relies on an email account and the free version tracks you for ads. On its US App Store page, checked 14 November 2026, it has a 4.7 rating from 386,264 ratings. Here's how it does on the checklist, from its own pages, checked 28 November 2026:

  • Encryption: its help center says files are protected with "256-bit AES encryption", that "all files are encrypted at rest", and that unlocking with your Keepsafe PIN is the only way to decrypt them (Keepsafe Support). It doesn't name the mode or say where the key comes from.
  • Account and recovery: signing in uses an access code sent to your account email, and a forgotten PIN is reset the same way. That's convenient, but your email account becomes a way into the vault.
  • Backup: the listing includes photo backup, and Premium adds Private Cloud for up to 10,000 items.
  • Privacy label: "Data Used to Track You" (usage data), and the listing says Keepsafe Basic is ad supported.

So Keepsafe is a reasonable choice if you want a well-known app with cloud backup and email recovery, and you're comfortable with ads and an account. If you'd rather have no account, see Keepsafe alternatives.

How do you use a photo vault more safely?

  • Use a long code, and don't reuse your iPhone passcode.
  • Save your recovery phrase or key on paper, somewhere safe and away from the phone.
  • Remove originals from Photos and Recently Deleted after importing, and check whether iCloud Photos still has a copy.
  • Test the backup before you need it, especially before changing phones.
  • Keep a second copy of anything irreplaceable, somewhere you control.

It doesn't protect photos you leave in Photos, and it can't help if someone sees you draw your pattern or finds your recovery phrase. Questions people ask

Is a photo vault safe?

It can be. A safe vault encrypts photos on your phone with a key made from your code, keeps a backup you can restore, and doesn't track you. A vault that only shows a PIN screen in front of normal files is much weaker. Run the 7 checks above before you trust one.

Is a photo vault secure against hackers?

A properly encrypted one is, as long as your code stays private. Weak vaults can leak photos through unencrypted files or a cloud account. With strong encryption, the weak points become your code, your recovery phrase and any email account that can reset the vault.

Is a vault app safe to keep on my phone?

Yes, if it passes the checklist. Having one installed tells people you have something private, so if someone might make you open it, set up a decoy vault and see how decoy vaults work.

Is Keepsafe photo vault safe?

Keepsafe says it encrypts files with 256-bit AES at rest, and it's widely used. The trade-offs are an email account that can reset your PIN, ads in the free version, and a privacy label that lists data used to track you.

How does a vault app work?

It keeps photos in its own storage, away from the Photos app, and asks for a code before showing them. Good vaults also encrypt each file with a key made from that code, so the files can't be read without it.

Can photo vault apps see my photos?

Not if the app encrypts on your phone with a key only you hold. If it keeps photos unencrypted, or stores them on its own servers with a key it controls, it could.
