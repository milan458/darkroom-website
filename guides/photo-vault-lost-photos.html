Guide · Missing photos

Your photo vault app lost your photos: what to do now

By Inertia Projects·Published 22 October 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Photo vault recovery only works from a copy that still exists, so don't delete, reinstall or reset anything yet: deleting an iPhone app deletes its data. Check the app's trash or a second vault, your old phone, the app's own cloud, your iCloud and any computer backup, in that order. Then email the developer with your app version and what happened.

You can often recover photos from a photo vault app, but only from a copy that still exists: a trash folder or second vault inside the app, your old phone, the app's own cloud, your iCloud, or a computer backup. If no copy exists anywhere, no app or service can bring them back. This guide is for iPhone owners whose vault photos went missing or were deleted, with recovery steps for Private Photo Vault, Keepsafe, Gallery Vault and other popular vaults.

Missing photos were one of the most common complaints when we read recent one- and two-star App Store reviews of eight vault apps in October 2026 (more on our about page). Often "missing" means "not where you're looking". Locked out rather than missing photos? See what to do if you forgot your photo vault password.

What should you do before you delete or reinstall the app?

Stop, and don't delete it yet. Apple says deleting an app "removes the app and its related data" (Apple: check iPhone storage), and a vault app's photos are that data. Reinstalling gives you an empty vault. More in does deleting a vault app delete your photos?

Before you delete, reinstall, reset or switch phones, do these in order:

  1. Check the app's backup. Open its settings and look for backup, sync or cloud. Note whether it's on, when it last finished, and which account it uses.
  2. Export anything that still opens. A plain copy in Photos or Files is better than no copy. See how to export photos from a vault app.
  3. Write down what a restore will need: the account email, any cloud or backup password, and your PIN, pattern or recovery phrase. Private Photo Vault, for example, says it can't recover a forgotten Cloud Vault password (Private Photo Vault FAQ).
  4. Leave the vault app in your iPhone backup. In Settings › [your name] › iCloud › Storage › Backups (or Manage Account Storage on some versions), tap this iPhone and keep the vault app switched on. Apple says turning an app off there deletes its backup data from iCloud (Apple: manage iCloud storage).
  5. Offload instead of deleting if you only need space. In Settings › General › iPhone Storage, Apple's Offload App "frees up storage used by the app, but keeps its documents and data" (Apple).
  6. Email the developer and wait for an answer. Private Photo Vault's FAQ asks for exactly this before you delete or reinstall.

And don't do these:

  • Don't restore an older iPhone backup over your phone yet. Private Photo Vault's FAQ warns that restoring a computer backup can replace the data on the phone now.
  • Don't pay for a "vault recovery" tool or service. Software can't decrypt a properly encrypted vault without its key. If the vault isn't encrypted, the developer can help for free.

Where could the photos still be?

Check these places in order. Most recoveries happen at one of the first three.

  1. Inside the app, but somewhere else. Look in other albums or folders, in a trash or "Recently Deleted" area (Keepsafe has a Trash, for example), and behind a second PIN. Private Photo Vault's listing includes a decoy password, and a decoy you set up and forgot opens a different set of photos. See what a decoy vault is.
  2. On your old iPhone or iPad. Some vaults keep photos only on the device you added them on. Secret Photo Vault's App Store listing says all its data is stored only on your device (App Store). Open the vault on the old phone before you erase it.
  3. In the app's cloud or your iCloud. Account-based vaults such as Keepsafe restore when you sign in to the same account. Vaults that back up to your private iCloud, such as Vaultaire, need the same Apple Account. The app-by-app steps are below.
  4. On a computer. Look for an iPhone backup made with Finder, Apple Devices or iTunes, or for copies you sent over Wi-Fi transfer.
  5. In Photos. Open Photos, swipe up, and look under Utilities › Recently Deleted. Apple keeps items there for 30 days (Apple: recover deleted photos). If you deleted the originals after importing them into the vault, they may still be there.

How do you recover photos in Private Photo Vault, Keepsafe, Gallery Vault and other apps?

Follow the steps for the app you used, because each one stores and backs up photos differently. All of this comes from each maker's own help pages or App Store listing, checked 28 November 2026.

Private Photo Vault

Private Photo Vault, by Legendary Software Labs, keeps photos on the phone unless you turned on Cloud Vault. Its App Store listing says the free version doesn't back up to Cloud Vault (App Store). From its FAQ:

  1. Cloud Vault was on and finished uploading: sign in on the phone and the files restore. You need your Cloud Vault password, which is separate from the app lock and which the company can't recover.
  2. You backed up the old iPhone to a computer with Finder, Apple Devices or iTunes: restore that backup to the new iPhone, then check your files before you clear the old one.
  3. You deleted the app and had neither: the FAQ says they can't restore deleted files without a usable backup.

The same FAQ notes that some customers have had trouble restoring vault data from iCloud device backups, so don't rely on those alone (Private Photo Vault FAQ).

Keepsafe

Keepsafe is account-based, so recovery runs through your Keepsafe email.

  1. New phone or reinstall: install Keepsafe, sign in to the same account, turn on Settings › Backup & Sync, and keep the app open with the screen on until syncing finishes (Keepsafe: transfer to a new phone).
  2. Deleted inside the app: go to Settings › Trash, select the items and tap Recover. Keepsafe says recovering from Trash needs Premium, and items removed from Trash can't be recovered (Keepsafe: recovering items from Trash).
  3. Lost access to your email: Keepsafe sends access codes only to a verified address, and says an account can't be recovered without one (Keepsafe Help Center).

Gallery Vault

Several different apps are called Gallery Vault, so check the maker's name on the app's store page first. The widely used Android GalleryVault has its help center run by ThinkYeah. Its steps:

  1. Open Gallery Vault's Settings and use Find Lost Files. This works if you haven't cleared the app's data or changed phones (ThinkYeah: recover lost files).
  2. Leave its hidden folder alone. ThinkYeah says not to delete, rename or move the folder that starts with .galleryvault_DoNotDelete, and to stop cleaning apps from removing it.
  3. Back up before a new phone or factory reset. Its Google Play listing says hidden files are stored only on your device, and ThinkYeah recommends linking Google Drive.

On iPhone, one app named "GalleryVault -Hide Photo Video" (DAOWEI Network Technology) has a developer reply on its App Store page saying files are cleaned up when the app is uninstalled (App Store). Other Gallery Vault apps on iPhone come from other makers, so use their own help pages.

How do you ask the developer for help?

Email the developer before you change anything else. Their contact details are on the app's App Store page under App Support, or in the app's settings. Include:

  • The app name and version, and your iPhone model and iOS version.
  • What you did just before the photos went missing (update, new phone, reinstall, subscription change).
  • Roughly how many items are missing, and whether thumbnails show.
  • Whether you used the app's cloud backup, and the account email if it has one.

Never send your PIN, password, pattern or recovery phrase. A real developer won't ask for it.

How do you stop it happening again?

Use a vault with a backup you can restore, check it now and then, and keep your own copy of anything you can't replace.

  • Know where your backup lives and what you need to restore it: an account, an Apple Account, a code, a recovery phrase.
  • Check that backups finish, and that your iCloud or the app's cloud still has space.
  • Export irreplaceable photos to an encrypted drive or a computer you control, as a second copy.
  • Before switching phones, follow our new-iPhone checklist.

Questions people ask

Is there a photo vault recovery app that works?

Not one that can open an encrypted vault. Recovery works by restoring a copy that already exists: the app's cloud, your iCloud, an old phone or a computer backup. Be wary of any tool that promises to unlock a vault, and ask the app's developer instead.

How do you recover deleted photos from a vault app?

Look in the app's trash or Recently Deleted area first, then in its cloud backup. If you deleted the whole app, only a backup can bring them back, because deleting an iPhone app removes its data.

Will reinstalling the vault app bring my photos back?

Only if the app restores them from a cloud backup. Reinstalling by itself gives you an empty vault. Contact the developer first, and offload rather than delete if you only need space.

Does an iCloud backup of my iPhone include my vault photos?

It depends on the app. Obscura says its vault is included when iCloud Backup is on, while Private Photo Vault says some customers have had trouble restoring vault data that way. Check the app's own settings or help pages.

Can the developer recover my vault photos?

Only from a backup they or you still have. Private Photo Vault, for example, says it can't restore deleted files without a usable backup. A developer that encrypts with your own key can't open your vault for you.

I see thumbnails but the photos won't open. Are they lost?

Not necessarily. The full-size files may still be syncing from the cloud. Keepsafe, for example, asks you to keep the app open with the screen on until syncing finishes. Stay on Wi-Fi and power, and check that your cloud storage isn't full.
