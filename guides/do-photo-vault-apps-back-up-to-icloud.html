Do photo vault apps back up to iCloud?

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 24 November 2026

Short answer

Some do, many don't. Of 15 vault apps we've reviewed, 3 back up to your own iCloud, a few use their own paid cloud, and the rest keep photos only on the phone. Your iPhone's iCloud Backup often doesn't cover vault apps either. Check the app's backup screen, and test a restore before you rely on it.

Most people assume that because their iPhone backs up to iCloud, their vault app does too. Often it doesn't. This guide explains the three kinds of vault backup, which apps use which, and how to check yours before you need it. If your photos are already missing, start with photo vault lost photos.

What are the ways a vault app can back up?

Three, and they're very different:

  • Your own iCloud. The app stores its data in your private iCloud space, through Apple's CloudKit. It counts toward your iCloud storage, and the developer doesn't hold it. Done well, it's encrypted on the phone first.
  • The developer's cloud. The app uploads to its own servers, usually with an account and often as a paid feature. You depend on the company staying in business and keeping it secure.
  • Nothing, or a file you make. Photos live only on the phone. You back up by exporting to a computer or drive yourself.

Doesn't my iPhone's iCloud Backup include vault apps?

Not reliably. iCloud Backup covers data that apps choose to include. Many vault apps exclude their files on purpose, so a restored iPhone gets the app back but an empty vault. Others include them, but then restoring needs the whole phone restored.

The only way to know is to read the app's own help pages or test it: restore to a spare device, or ask the developer.

How do you check your vault's backup works?

Find the backup screen, confirm it's up to date, and know how you'd restore.

  1. Open the app's settings and look for Backup, Cloud or Sync.
  2. Check the last backup time and that nothing is waiting to upload.
  3. Check where it goes: your iCloud, the developer's cloud, or nowhere.
  4. Check what restores it: your PIN, an account login, or a recovery code. Write it down somewhere safe.
  5. Check your iCloud storage in Settings › [your name] › iCloud. A full iCloud stops backups.
  6. Keep a second copy of anything irreplaceable, exported to a drive you control.

What should you ask about a vault's own cloud?

A backup on the developer's servers can work well, but you're trusting the company with your photos. Before you rely on one, find out:

  • Is it encrypted on your phone first, so the company can't see your photos?
  • What happens if you stop paying? Some cloud plans delete or lock photos when a subscription ends.
  • Can you download everything at full size, or only compressed copies?
  • What happens if the company shuts down? Keep your own export of anything irreplaceable either way.

Is a backup in your own iCloud private?

Only if the app encrypts it on the phone first. Your iCloud storage is protected by Apple, but with standard data protection Apple holds the keys to most iCloud data. Questions people ask

Do photo vault apps back up to iCloud?

Some do. Of 15 apps we reviewed, Vaultaire, Encamera (with Premium) and Noxium back up to your own iCloud. Several use their own cloud, and many keep photos only on the phone.

Will my vault restore from an iPhone backup?

Not always. Many vault apps leave their files out of the iPhone's iCloud Backup. Check the app's help pages before you erase or replace your phone.

Does a vault backup use my iCloud storage?

If it backs up to your own iCloud, yes, it counts toward your iCloud plan. Backups to the developer's cloud don't.

Can Apple see my vault backup?

Not if the app encrypts it on the phone before uploading. Otherwise, with standard iCloud protection, Apple holds the keys.
