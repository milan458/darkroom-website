Feature

Shared vaults: one encrypted vault for you and one other person

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 17 November 2026

Short answer

A shared vault in Noxium Pro is a separate encrypted vault you share with one person through iCloud. It opens with a password of 10 or more characters that you give them in person, never with your pattern, and it never touches your other vaults.

Couples, families and friends sometimes need a private place for photos they both keep: documents, a child's medical photos, or pictures meant for just the two of you. Sending them over a chat app leaves copies everywhere. Noxium, the photo vault we make, lets you share one vault instead. This page covers how it works, how to set it up, and the risks of sharing anything private.

How does a shared vault work?

It's a vault of its own, stored in your iCloud and shared through Apple's iCloud sharing. A password opens it, not a pattern.

  1. Its own key. The shared vault's key is made from the password and a salt of its own, through Argon2id. Your pattern and your other vaults play no part.
  2. Encrypted before upload. Like every Noxium vault, its photos, videos and notes are encrypted on the device with AES-256-GCM, so iCloud only holds encrypted data. See on-device encryption.
  3. Shared through iCloud. Noxium uses Apple's iCloud sharing to invite the other person. They need Noxium and an Apple Account with iCloud.
  4. The password is the key. An invitation alone doesn't open anything. The other person also needs the password, which you give them face to face.
  5. Separate from everything else. Joining your shared vault doesn't give the other person any way into your main vault, your decoy vault or your pattern.

How do you create and share a vault?

Go to Settings › Vaults › Shared vault, set a name and password, then tap Create and invite.

  1. Open your main vault and tap Settings. Under Vaults, tap Shared vault. It needs Pro.
  2. Name the vault and type a password of at least 10 characters, twice. It can't be one that already opens another vault.
  3. Tap Create and invite. Apple's iCloud sharing screen opens. Choose how to send the invitation.
  4. Tell the other person the password in person. Don't send it in the same message as the invitation.
  5. The other person opens the invitation. Noxium shows "Someone shared a Noxium vault with you." They tap Enter password.
  6. Either of you opens it later from the lock screen, with Open a shared vault.

When is a shared vault a good idea?

When two people you trust both need the same private photos, and both of you have an iPhone or iPad.

  • Partners who keep photos meant only for each other.
  • Family documents: passports, insurance cards, medical photos for a child.
  • A carer or relative who needs a few documents in an emergency.

What doesn't a shared vault protect?

Encryption protects the vault from outsiders. It can't control what the other person does once they've opened it.

  • The other person's choices. Anyone who can open the vault can view, export or screenshot what's in it. Only share with someone you trust with those photos.
  • A relationship that ends. Stopping sharing later doesn't remove copies someone already saved. Think about this before you add intimate photos.
  • A weak or leaked password. Use a long password you haven't used anywhere else, and never send it with the invitation.
  • Someone who has both. A person with the invitation link and the password can open the vault. Treat them like a key and the address together.

If photos of you have been shared without your consent, StopNCII.org can help stop them spreading on participating platforms.

Questions people ask

Is there a shared photo vault app for couples?

Yes. Noxium Pro can share one encrypted vault with one other person through iCloud. It opens with a password you give them in person, and your other vaults stay private.

Can the other person see my main vault?

No. The shared vault has its own key, made from its own password. It gives no way into your pattern or your other vaults.

Does the other person need Noxium?

Yes, on an iPhone or iPad with iCloud turned on. They open your invitation in Noxium and enter the password you gave them.

Is a shared vault safer than sending photos in a chat app?

It keeps the photos in one encrypted place instead of in chat history and backups on both phones. It can't stop the other person saving copies. See are photo vault apps safe?
