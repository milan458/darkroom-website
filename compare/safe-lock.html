Safe Lock review: how it compares with Noxium

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

Safe Lock - Hidden Photo Vault is a highly rated, private vault with many lock types, a fake password that opens a fake gallery, break-in photos, shake-to-lock and a Data Not Collected label. Its listing names no encryption and no automatic backup: files stay on the phone unless you export them to a cloud yourself. Pick Safe Lock for lock options and a fake gallery; pick Noxium for named encryption and an encrypted iCloud backup.

Safe Lock - Hidden Photo Vault, by developer Katerina Akhlebinina, has been on the App Store since March 2017 and has 24,991 US ratings at 4.8 stars, one of the highest scores of any vault app we've checked. Its listing also calls it "Safe Lock - Private Photo Vault". Everything here comes from its App Store listing, checked on 21 November 2026, compared with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly.

What does Safe Lock do well, and where does it fall short?

Safe Lock is well loved, private by its label and full of lock features. Its gaps are encryption and backup.

What Safe Lock does well

  • Very well rated. 4.8 stars from 24,991 US ratings.
  • Data Not Collected. The listing says the developer has no access to your secrets, and photos and videos are saved only on your device.
  • A fake gallery. A fake password opens a harmless gallery instead of your real one.
  • Many ways to lock. PIN, dot lock, numeric or letter passwords, Touch ID and Face ID, plus shaking or flipping the phone to lock and hide the app at once.
  • Break-in reports. It takes a photo of someone who tries the wrong code.
  • Lots of ways to move files. Wi-Fi transfer, and import or export with Dropbox, Google Drive, Yandex.Disk, iCloud and Box.
  • Extras. A hidden camera with zoom, notes, GIF and PDF support, slideshows, a custom icon and dark mode. It runs on iOS 12 and later.

Where Safe Lock falls short

  • No encryption named. The listing describes locks and secret folders, not encrypted files.
  • No automatic backup. Files stay on the phone; you have to export them to a cloud yourself to keep a copy.
  • No recovery in the listing. It doesn't say what happens if you forget your code.
  • Unclear plans. All 3 purchases are called "Premium Account" ($4.99, $19.99 and $29.99), so you see what each covers only in the app.

How are Safe Lock and Noxium different?

Both keep your photos away from their developer and have a decoy. The differences are encryption, backup and recovery.

Encryption

Noxium encrypts every photo, video and note on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your pattern through Argon2id; names, dates and places are encrypted too. Safe Lock's listing doesn't mention encryption. A lock screen keeps out someone holding your phone; encryption also protects files copied off it another way. See how encrypted photo vaults work.

Backup

Safe Lock leaves backup to you: export to a cloud service, where copies may no longer be locked. Noxium backs up only encrypted data to your own iCloud, free and automatically, and restores on a new iPhone when you draw your pattern; see encrypted iCloud backup.

Decoys and intruders

Safe Lock's fake password opens a fake gallery, and it photographs failed unlock attempts. Noxium has a second pattern for a separate decoy vault, free, and Pro adds an emergency erase pattern and Calculator, Notes or Weather lookalike icons.

Getting back in

Safe Lock's listing doesn't describe recovery. Noxium can't reset your pattern for you; you write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault on this iPhone or a new one.

Price

Safe Lock lists three "Premium Account" purchases, at $4.99, $19.99 and $29.99. Noxium's free plan includes 150 photos, 10 videos, unlimited notes and full backup; Pro is $2.99 a week, $17.99 a year or $39.99 once.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Safe Lock if you want many lock types, a fake gallery, break-in photos and shake-to-lock, need it on an older iPhone, and are happy to back up by exporting.
  • Pick Noxium if you want named encryption, an automatic encrypted backup that survives a lost phone, and a recovery phrase.

How do you switch from Safe Lock without losing photos?

Export the photos, check the copies, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything.

  1. Count what you have in each secret folder, and in the fake gallery if you want to keep it.
  2. Export photos and videos to your Photo Roll, or to a computer with Wi-Fi transfer.
  3. Check the copies: counts match, videos play, photos open at full size.
  4. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos. See import and export.
  5. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup.
  6. Delete any copies you exported to Dropbox, Google Drive or another cloud, if you no longer need them.
  7. Only then delete Safe Lock, and cancel Premium in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in your Photos library until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

A vault that survives a lost phone

Noxium backs up an encrypted copy of your vault to your own iCloud, automatically. On a new iPhone, draw your pattern and it restores.

Questions people ask

Is Safe Lock safe?

Its privacy label is Data Not Collected and it keeps files on your device. Its listing doesn't mention encryption or an automatic backup. See are photo vault apps safe?

Does Safe Lock have a fake password?

Yes. Its listing says a fake password opens a fake gallery. See what a decoy vault is and how to test one.

Does Safe Lock back up to iCloud?

You can export or import files with iCloud, Dropbox, Google Drive, Yandex.Disk or Box, but the listing doesn't describe an automatic backup.

How much is Safe Lock Premium?

On the US App Store in November 2026, three "Premium Account" purchases were listed: $4.99, $19.99 and $29.99. The app shows what each covers.

What are Safe Lock break-in reports?

When someone enters the wrong code, Safe Lock takes their photo so you can see who tried to get in.
