Vaultaire review: how it compares with Noxium in 2026

By Inertia Projects·Published 14 November 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Vaultaire is a well-built, encrypted vault: AES-256-GCM on the device, a 5×5 pattern lock, no account, decoy vaults and an encrypted iCloud backup on its free plan. Its reviews are good but few: 72 US ratings averaging 4.7. Noxium, which we make, works the same way on those points. Pick Vaultaire for 3 free vaults, 31 languages and family plans; pick Noxium for a bigger free plan, Argon2id key stretching, cheaper yearly Pro and a Data Not Collected privacy label.

Vaultaire is a good, honest photo vault: it encrypts on the iPhone, needs no account, and backs up to your own iCloud, even on its free plan. Its one weak spot is age. It is new, with 72 US ratings averaging 4.7 when we checked on 28 November 2026 (App Store). This review is for iPhone users who found Vaultaire, or vaultaire.app, and want to know whether it's worth trusting with their photos and what the alternatives are.

Of all the vaults we compare, Vaultaire is the one most like Noxium: both encrypt on the iPhone, both open with a pattern on a 5×5 grid, and both back up to your own iCloud without an account. That makes the differences smaller and more technical. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly. What does Vaultaire do well, and where does it fall short?

Vaultaire gets the important things right: real encryption on the device, no account, a free encrypted backup and decoys. Its trade-offs are a smaller free plan, a key derivation that needs little memory, a recovery sentence you can view again, and some data collection.

What Vaultaire does well

  • Encryption it explains. The listing names AES-256-GCM, and its website describes how the pattern becomes a key, that a random master key encrypts file headers, content, thumbnails and metadata, and how backups are sealed before CloudKit receives them (vaultaire.app). Few vaults publish this much.
  • It says what it can't hide. Its website warns that an examiner with a copy of the app's files can still estimate how many vaults you have (vaultaire.app). That kind of honesty is rare.
  • No account, no ads, no tracking. The listing says "No account is required" and "No ads or cross-app tracking", and its privacy label lists no data used to track you.
  • Free backup. Encrypted iCloud backup is included on the free plan and turned on by default.
  • 3 vaults on the free plan, so a decoy plus two more without paying.
  • Family plans. Pro for your Apple family, at $49.99 a year or $99.99 for life. Noxium doesn't sell one.
  • 31 languages, and import from Files and by drag and drop as well as from Photos.

Where Vaultaire falls short

  • Few reviews so far. 72 ratings is a small sample. The average is good, but there isn't much history yet.
  • A smaller free plan: 100 photos and 10 videos in total, across all vaults.
  • PBKDF2 rather than a memory-hard function. 600,000 iterations of PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 is a standard, reasonable choice, but PBKDF2 needs almost no memory. Argon2, which Noxium uses, is a memory-hard function (RFC 9106): every guess has to use a lot of memory, which makes guessing in bulk more expensive.
  • The recovery sentence can be viewed later. Vaultaire's own page says anyone who can open the vault and reach its settings may be able to see it (vaultaire.app).
  • Some data collected. Its privacy label links your email address and support messages to you if you contact the developer, and collects usage and crash data not linked to you.
  • Optimized imports by default. Vaultaire's own guide says imports default to Optimized, which can convert or downscale some photos and videos. Choose Original in Settings to keep files as they are (vaultaire.app).

How are Vaultaire and Noxium different?

On the big things they match: no account, AES-256-GCM on the device, a 5×5 pattern, decoys, and a free encrypted backup in your own iCloud. The differences are in how the pattern becomes a key, how recovery works, the free plan, price and data collection.

How the pattern becomes a key

Noxium stretches your pattern with Argon2id (libsodium), and the pattern itself is never stored. Vaultaire uses PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 with 600,000 iterations and a device-wide salt, and, its site says, keeps recovery information, including the pattern, inside an AES-GCM encrypted recovery database in the iOS Keychain. Both are sound designs; the difference is how costly each guess is for someone with a copy of your files. For the background, see how encrypted photo vaults work and our pattern lock page. Neither protects you if someone watches you draw your pattern.

Recovery

Both give you a phrase in case you forget your pattern. Noxium has you write down a recovery phrase at setup, and nobody can reset a pattern, not us and not Apple. Vaultaire's is a 9-word generated sentence, or a custom one of at least 6 words, that you can view again later in the vault's settings. Vaultaire says that on a new device the phrase restores only from an encrypted iCloud backup. Viewable later is easier if you lose your paper copy; not viewable is safer if someone gets into your open vault. More on ours: recovery phrase.

Decoys and panic options

Both open a decoy vault with a second pattern. With Pro, both have a pattern for when you're forced to open the app: Noxium's emergency erase opens a harmless vault and erases the others; Vaultaire's duress mode opens one chosen vault and removes the other vaults from the device, while its encrypted cloud backups stay available for recovery with the phrase. Read what a decoy vault is for the limits of both.

Free plan and price

Noxium's free plan holds 150 photos, 10 videos and unlimited notes, with 1 decoy vault. Vaultaire's holds 100 photos and 10 videos across up to 3 vaults. For yearly Pro, Vaultaire charges $19.99 on its US App Store page. Noxium Pro is $17.99 a year (our pricing). Both sell lifetime Pro for $39.99. Vaultaire's weekly plan is cheaper at $1.99 a week. If Noxium Pro ends, you keep full access to view, export and back up everything already in your vault.

Privacy label

Noxium has no ads, no accounts and no analytics, and its label is Data Not Collected. Vaultaire lists no tracking but does list data linked to you and data not linked to you.

Which should you choose?

Both are reasonable picks. Choose on the details.

  • Pick Vaultaire if you want 3 vaults without paying, a family plan, a recovery sentence you can look up again, or the app in one of its 31 languages.
  • Pick Noxium if you want a bigger free plan, Argon2id key stretching, a pattern that is never stored, a cheaper yearly Pro, and a Data Not Collected label.

Looking for other Vaultaire alternatives? Obscura and Encamera also need no account; our no-account vault comparison lists their prices and backups side by side.

How do you switch from Vaultaire without losing photos?

Export to Photos, check the copies, import into the new vault, and only then delete anything.

  1. Check your import setting. If photos went into Vaultaire under its Optimized setting, the export brings back the compressed copies, not the originals. Compare a few with the originals if you still have them.
  2. Export each vault to Photos, one at a time, including any decoy you want to keep: unlock the vault, tap Select, pick the items and choose Export or Save to Photos. Note how many items each holds.
  3. Check the copies: counts match, videos play, dates look right. Exports usually return to their capture date, so look further back in your library.
  4. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos.
  5. Wait for Noxium's backup to finish in Settings › iCloud backup.
  6. Only then delete from Vaultaire, including its iCloud backup if you no longer want it, and cancel Pro in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos are unencrypted in your library until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

Questions people ask

Are Vaultaire's reviews good?

Yes, but there are few of them. On 28 November 2026 its US App Store page showed 72 ratings averaging 4.7. That's a good start for a new app, not a long track record.

Is vaultaire.app the official Vaultaire website?

It appears to be. The site's footer names Wraxle LLC, the same developer as Vaultaire's App Store listing, and it describes the same app: no account, a 5×5 pattern and encrypted iCloud backup.

What are the alternatives to Vaultaire?

Noxium, which we make, works the most like it: no account, a drawn pattern, AES-256-GCM and an encrypted backup to your own iCloud. Obscura and Encamera also need no account. Keepsafe is the better-known option, with an email account and its own cloud.

Is Vaultaire safe?

It encrypts files on the device with AES-256-GCM, needs no account, and seals its iCloud backup before upload. Like any vault, it's only as strong as your pattern, so use a long one with several turns.

Is Vaultaire free?

Yes, for up to 3 vaults, 100 photos and 10 videos in total, with encrypted iCloud backup. Pro starts at $1.99 a week on the US App Store; lifetime is $39.99.

What if I forget my Vaultaire pattern?

Use the recovery sentence from setup. Vaultaire says it has no account reset or provider-held key, so if you lose both the pattern and the sentence, it can't recover the vault for you.
