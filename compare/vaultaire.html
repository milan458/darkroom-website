Vaultaire review: how it compares with Noxium in 2026

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 14 November 2026

Short answer

Vaultaire is a well-built, encrypted vault: AES-256-GCM, a 5×5 pattern lock, no account, decoy vaults and an encrypted iCloud backup on its free plan. Noxium works the same way on those points. Pick Vaultaire for 3 free vaults, 31 languages and family plans; pick Noxium for a bigger free plan, Argon2id key stretching, cheaper yearly Pro and a Data Not Collected privacy label.

Vaultaire, from Wraxle LLC, came to the App Store in March 2026 and has 65 US ratings at 4.7 stars. Of all the vaults we compare, it's the one most like Noxium: both encrypt on the iPhone, both open with a pattern on a 5×5 grid, and both back up to your own iCloud without an account. That makes the differences smaller and more technical. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly. Everything about Vaultaire here comes from its App Store listing (checked 14 November 2026) and its own feature pages.

What does Vaultaire do well, and where does it fall short?

Vaultaire gets the important things right: real encryption on the device, no account, a free encrypted backup and decoys. Its trade-offs are a smaller free plan, a faster-to-compute key derivation, and some data collection.

What Vaultaire does well

  • Encryption it explains. The listing names AES-256-GCM, and its website describes how the pattern becomes a key and how backups are protected. Few vaults publish this much.
  • No account, no ads, no tracking. Its privacy label lists no data used to track you, and the listing says there are no ads.
  • Free backup. Encrypted iCloud backup is included on the free plan and turned on by default.
  • 3 vaults on the free plan, so a decoy plus two more without paying.
  • Family plans. Pro for your Apple family, at $49.99 a year or $99.99 for life. Noxium doesn't sell one.
  • 31 languages, against Noxium's 16, and import from Files and by drag and drop as well as from Photos.
  • A head start. Eight months on the App Store, with ratings and reviews you can read.

Where Vaultaire falls short

  • A smaller free plan: 100 photos and 10 videos in total, across all vaults.
  • PBKDF2 rather than a memory-hard function. 600,000 iterations of PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 is a reasonable, standard choice, but it uses almost no memory, so it is cheaper to run in bulk on graphics cards than Argon2id, which is designed to need a lot of memory for every guess.
  • The recovery sentence can be viewed later from the open vault's settings, which Vaultaire's own page notes means anyone who gets into the vault may see it.
  • Some data collected. Its privacy label lists data linked to you and data not linked to you; it doesn't say "Data Not Collected".
  • Optimized imports by default. Vaultaire's own guide says imports default to an Optimized setting that can convert or downscale photos and videos, with an Original setting you can choose instead.

How are Vaultaire and Noxium different?

On the big things they match: no account, AES-256-GCM on the device, a 5×5 pattern, decoys, and a free encrypted backup in your own iCloud. The differences are in how the pattern becomes a key, how recovery works, the free plan, price and data collection.

How the pattern becomes a key

Noxium stretches your pattern with Argon2id using 64 MiB of memory and 3 passes, and the pattern itself is never stored. Vaultaire uses PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 with 600,000 iterations and, its site says, keeps the pattern inside an encrypted database in the iOS Keychain. Both are sound designs; Argon2id is the newer standard because the memory it needs makes mass guessing more expensive. For the background, see how encrypted photo vaults work and our pattern lock page.

Recovery

Both give you a phrase in case you forget your pattern, and both work on a new iPhone through the iCloud backup. Noxium's is 8 random words from the BIP-39 list (or 6–9 words of your own), shown once at setup and never stored, so it can't be shown again; you can make a new one in Settings. Vaultaire's is a 9-word generated sentence that you can view again later in settings. One is safer if someone gets into your vault; the other is easier if you lose your paper copy. More on ours: recovery phrase.

Decoys and panic options

Both open a decoy vault with a second pattern. With Pro, both have a pattern for when you're forced to open the app: Noxium's emergency erase opens a harmless vault and erases the others; Vaultaire's duress vault removes access to the other vaults on that device. Read what a decoy vault is for the limits of both.

Free plan and price

Noxium's free plan holds 150 photos, 10 videos and unlimited notes, with 1 decoy vault. Vaultaire's holds 100 photos and 10 videos across up to 3 vaults. For Pro, Vaultaire's yearly plan is $19.99; Noxium's is $17.99. Both sell lifetime Pro for $39.99. Vaultaire's weekly plan is cheaper: $1.99, against Noxium's $2.99. If Noxium Pro ends, you keep full access to view, export and back up everything already in your vault.

Privacy label

Noxium has no ads, no accounts and no analytics, and its label is Data Not Collected. Vaultaire lists no tracking but does list data linked to you and data not linked to you.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Vaultaire if you want 3 vaults without paying, a family plan, the app in a language Noxium doesn't offer yet, or an app with a few months of reviews behind it.
  • Pick Noxium if you want a bigger free plan, Argon2id key stretching, a recovery phrase that is never stored, a cheaper yearly Pro, and a Data Not Collected label.

How do you switch from Vaultaire without losing photos?

Export to Photos, check the copies, import into the new vault, and only then delete anything. Vaultaire says you can export files back into Photos.

  1. Check your import setting. If photos went into Vaultaire under its Optimized setting, the export can't bring back the original size. Compare a few with the originals if you still have them.
  2. Export each vault to Photos, one at a time, including any decoy you want to keep. Note how many items each holds.
  3. Check the copies: counts match, videos play, dates look right.
  4. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos.
  5. Wait for Noxium's backup to finish in Settings › iCloud backup.
  6. Only then delete from Vaultaire, including its iCloud backup if you no longer want it, and cancel Pro in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos are unencrypted in your library until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

Questions people ask

Is Vaultaire safe?

It encrypts files on the device with AES-256-GCM, needs no account, and its iCloud backup is encrypted before upload. Like any vault, it's only as strong as your pattern, so use a long one.

Is Vaultaire free?

Yes, for up to 3 vaults, 100 photos and 10 videos, with encrypted iCloud backup. Pro starts at $1.99 a week on the US App Store; lifetime is $39.99.

Does Vaultaire need an account?

No. It works without an account, and its backups go to your own iCloud. Noxium works the same way.

What if I forget my Vaultaire pattern?

Use the 9-word recovery sentence from setup. Vaultaire says that if you lose both the pattern and the sentence, nobody can restore the vault.

Vaultaire or Keepsafe?

They're built differently: Vaultaire has no account and backs up to your iCloud; Keepsafe uses an email account and its own cloud. See Vaultaire vs Keepsafe.
