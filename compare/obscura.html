Obscura review: how it compares with Noxium

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

Obscura is one of the most careful vaults on the App Store: AES-256-GCM, open-source encryption code, no accounts, no cloud and a Data Not Collected label, plus encrypted document scanning and 10 password-separated vault slots. It has no cloud backup of its own; you back up with an encrypted export file or iOS's iCloud backup. Pick Obscura for open code and documents; pick Noxium for a pattern lock, a recovery phrase and an encrypted backup in your own iCloud.

Obscura: Encrypted Photo Vault, by Orlio Inc, came to the App Store in March 2026 and has 30 US ratings at 4.4 stars. It's young and small, but it's one of the few vaults that publishes the code that encrypts your files, along with an honest list of that code's limits. Everything here comes from its App Store listing and its ObscuraCrypto code page, checked on 21 November 2026, compared with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly.

What does Obscura do well, and where does it fall short?

Obscura gets the hard parts right and explains them openly. Its gaps are backup, recovery and a small free plan.

What Obscura does well

  • Named, checkable encryption. AES-256-GCM for every photo, video, document and database, and the encryption code is published on GitHub with tests you can run.
  • Honest about limits. Its code page explains that PBKDF2 is weaker against graphics-card guessing than newer methods like Argon2, and says so plainly.
  • Data Not Collected. No accounts, no analytics, and no internet connection needed.
  • Documents done well. Scan passports, IDs and receipts, search them by their text, get expiry reminders, and share a "Safe Copy" with parts blacked out.
  • Hidden vault slots. 10 slots always exist, each opening with its own password, with no list or count, so a second vault works as a decoy.
  • Fair when you stop paying. If a subscription ends, you can still view and export everything. Export is free on every plan.
  • Actively updated. Version 1.22.0 came out on 19 November 2026.

Where Obscura falls short

  • No cloud backup of its own. You export an encrypted file and store it yourself, or rely on iOS's own iCloud backup of the phone.
  • No way back in. There's no password reset or recovery phrase. Forget the password and the vault is gone.
  • A small free plan. 50 photos and documents in one vault.
  • Pricier subscriptions. $6.99 a month or $29.99 a year.
  • Needs iOS 18.2. Older iPhones that can't update are left out.

How are Obscura and Noxium different?

Both encrypt on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, collect no data and have no account. The differences are how your code becomes a key, backup, recovery and open code.

Encryption and open code

Noxium stretches your pattern into a key with Argon2id, which needs a lot of memory for every guess, and encrypts names, dates and places too. Obscura uses PBKDF2 with 600,000 rounds, which meets current OWASP guidance and is built into Apple's own code. Obscura's encryption code is public; Noxium's isn't, and publishes its design on the on-device encryption page instead. See how encrypted photo vaults work for what these terms mean.

Backup

Obscura deliberately has no cloud: you export one encrypted .obscura file and keep it on a computer, drive or cloud service you choose. Noxium backs up only encrypted data to your own iCloud, free and automatically, and restores on a new iPhone when you draw your pattern; see encrypted iCloud backup.

Decoys

Obscura's 10 hidden slots each open with their own password, so any one can be a decoy. Noxium has a second pattern for a separate decoy vault, free, and Pro adds an emergency erase pattern.

Getting back in

Neither developer can reset your code. Obscura has no recovery at all. Noxium gives you a recovery phrase at setup, which opens your vault if you forget your pattern.

Documents

Obscura is far ahead for documents: scanning, text search, expiry reminders and redacted copies. Noxium keeps photos, videos and encrypted notes, and imports HEIC, ProRAW and Live Photos in full quality.

Price

Obscura Pro is $6.99 a month, $29.99 a year or $39.99 lifetime, with a one-week trial on the yearly plan. Noxium's free plan includes 150 photos, 10 videos, unlimited notes and full backup; Pro is $2.99 a week, $17.99 a year or $39.99 once.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Obscura if you want to read the encryption code, you store IDs and documents as well as photos, and you're happy to manage your own encrypted backup file.
  • Pick Noxium if you want a pattern lock, a recovery phrase in case you forget, an automatic encrypted backup in your own iCloud, and a bigger free plan.

How do you switch from Obscura without losing photos?

Export a fresh encrypted backup first, export the photos, check them, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything.

  1. Export an encrypted .obscura backup and keep it until you're done. It's your safety net.
  2. Open each vault slot you use and count what's in it, including folders with their own password.
  3. Export photos and videos to Photos or Files.
  4. Check the copies: counts match, videos play, Live Photos move.
  5. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos. See import and export.
  6. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup.
  7. Only then delete the items from Obscura, and cancel Pro in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in Photos or Files until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using. Noxium doesn't store documents, so keep scanned IDs in Obscura or another safe place.

Encrypted, with a way back in

Noxium encrypts on your iPhone, backs up only ciphertext to your own iCloud, and gives you a recovery phrase in case you forget your pattern.

Questions people ask

Is Obscura safe?

It names AES-256-GCM, publishes its encryption code, and collects no data. Its main risk is losing access: there's no password reset, so keep the password and an export file safe. See are photo vault apps safe?

Is Obscura open source?

Its encryption code, ObscuraCrypto, is on GitHub. The app's screens, database and Face ID handling are not part of it, as its code page explains.

Does Obscura back up to iCloud?

Not to a cloud of its own. Its listing lists iOS system iCloud backup, and you can export the whole vault as one encrypted file to keep anywhere.

What if I forget my Obscura password?

The listing says there's no password reset, by design, and the developer can't open your vault. Read our guide to a forgotten vault password before you delete anything.

How much is Obscura Pro?

On the US App Store in November 2026: $6.99 a month, $29.99 a year, or $39.99 for Lifetime.
