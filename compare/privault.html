Privault review: how it compares with Noxium in 2026

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 14 November 2026

Short answer

Privault is a popular, long-running vault with a keypad lock, a second passcode for a decoy folder and an erase-all passcode. Its App Store listing doesn't name a cipher or describe any backup, and the app hasn't been updated since 28 February 2025. Pick Privault for its media player and Wi-Fi transfer; pick Noxium if you want named encryption, an encrypted backup in your own iCloud and no data used to track you.

Privault, from Binspir Technologies, has been on the App Store since January 2017 and has 76,566 US ratings at 4.7 stars. It's one of the vaults people find first when they search for a private photo vault. This review looks at what Privault offers, using its App Store listing checked on 14 November 2026, and compares it point by point with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about it accordingly; everything about Privault below comes from Privault's own listing.

What does Privault do well, and where does it fall short?

Privault does a lot with media and offers three passcodes for different situations. Its weak points are what the listing leaves out: the cipher, a backup, and recovery.

What Privault does well

  • Three passcodes. The primary passcode opens your files. A secondary passcode opens a separate folder, which the listing suggests giving out if someone threatens you. An erase-all passcode deletes all hidden files when you decide you have to.
  • Media support. The listing says its player handles about 150 video formats, including MKV, AVI and WMV, and its photo browser reads HEIC, TIFF and more.
  • A built-in voice recorder for private audio notes, set up from the keypad.
  • Wi-Fi transfer. An embedded Wi-Fi server lets you move files to and from a computer, as well as through the Camera Roll.
  • A one-time price. Permanent Pro is $45.99, so you don't have to subscribe.
  • Old devices. It runs on iOS 11 and later and supports iPad.

Where Privault falls short

  • No named encryption. The listing says the "encrypted storage" opens with your passcode, but it doesn't say how files are encrypted or how the passcode becomes a key. A short digit passcode is also far easier to guess than a long one, unless the app slows guessing down.
  • No backup described. The listing doesn't mention iCloud or any cloud backup. If the phone is lost or the app is deleted, assume the only copies were on that phone, unless you exported them.
  • No recovery described. The listing doesn't say what happens if you forget the passcode.
  • Tracking. Its privacy label lists data used to track you and data linked to you.
  • Not recently updated. The last update was 28 February 2025, more than 20 months before we checked. That isn't proof of a problem, but long gaps between updates can matter for an app that holds private files.

How are Privault and Noxium different?

The real differences are in what's documented: how files are encrypted, where the backup lives, and what happens when you forget your code.

Encryption

Noxium names every piece. Your pattern is turned into a key with Argon2id, and every photo, video and note is encrypted on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, including names, dates and places. The pattern itself is never stored. Our page on on-device encryption lists every layer. Privault's listing says its storage is encrypted, and stops there. If encryption matters to you, ask any vault to name its cipher; how encrypted photo vaults work explains what to look for.

Backup and moving to a new phone

Noxium keeps an encrypted backup in your own private iCloud, on the free plan too. On a new iPhone you draw your pattern and the vault restores. Privault's listing describes no backup; it offers export to the Camera Roll and a Wi-Fi server for copying files to a computer. That works as a manual backup, but the copies leave the vault unencrypted.

Decoys and panic erase

Both apps have a second code for a harmless space. Privault's secondary passcode opens a secondary folder; Noxium's second pattern opens a separate decoy vault, and the free plan includes 1. Both also have a way to erase: Privault's erase-all passcode, and Noxium's emergency erase pattern (Pro), which opens a harmless vault and erases the others. Read what a decoy vault is before you rely on one.

Forgetting your code

Noxium has no reset, by design. At setup you write down a recovery phrase, and it opens your vault if you forget your pattern. Privault's listing doesn't describe recovery, so contact its developer (support@binspir.com) before you need it.

Price and privacy

Both are free to start, with a one-time option. Privault's Permanent Pro is $45.99; Noxium Pro is $39.99 once. Noxium has no ads, no accounts and no analytics, and its privacy label is Data Not Collected. Privault sells a $2.99 purchase to remove ads, and its label lists data used to track you.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Privault if you need to play many video formats, move files over Wi-Fi to a computer, record private voice notes, or use an iPhone older than iOS 17.
  • Pick Noxium if you want encryption that's named and documented, an encrypted backup in your own iCloud that restores with your pattern, a recovery phrase, and no ads or tracking.

How do you switch from Privault without losing photos?

Export from Privault, check the copies, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything. Privault's listing says you can export through the Camera Roll or its Wi-Fi server.

  1. Count what you have. Note how many photos and videos are in each Privault folder, including the secondary folder.
  2. Export to the Camera Roll from inside Privault, folder by folder. For large videos, the Wi-Fi server to a computer may be quicker.
  3. Check the copies in Photos: counts match, videos play, quality is full size.
  4. Import into Noxium. Turn on Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos so it offers to remove the copies from Photos after import. Then empty Recently Deleted in Photos.
  5. Check the backup in Settings › iCloud backup: nothing waiting.
  6. Only then delete files from Privault, and cancel any subscription in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in your Photos library until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using. More tips: moving a vault without losing photos.

Questions people ask

Is Privault safe?

It hides files behind a passcode and says its storage is encrypted, but its listing doesn't name the cipher, describe a backup or explain recovery, and it was last updated on 28 February 2025. Our checklist in are photo vault apps safe? covers what to check.

Is Privault free?

Yes, to download. On the US App Store, Pro costs $3.49 a month, $24.99 a year or $45.99 once, and removing ads is a $2.99 purchase.

Does Privault back up to iCloud?

Its App Store listing doesn't mention iCloud or any cloud backup. It describes export to the Camera Roll and a Wi-Fi server for copying files to a computer.

What is Privault's secondary passcode?

A second passcode that opens a separate folder instead of your main files. The listing suggests giving it out if someone forces you to open the app.

What if I forget my Privault passcode?

The listing doesn't describe recovery. Don't delete the app, and contact the developer at support@binspir.com. Our guide to a forgotten vault password explains what's safe to try.
