MaxVault review: how it compares with Noxium

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 21 November 2026

Short answer

MaxVault is a "super app": a file vault, private browser and downloader, media player, editor and scanner, with folder locks, several vaults on different passcodes, break-in alerts and cloud sync to iCloud Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive. Its listing names no encryption, and its privacy label includes Data Linked to You. Pick MaxVault for features and file types; pick Noxium for named encryption and an encrypted backup in your own iCloud.

Photo & Video Vault - MaxVault, by Rambax, LLC, has been on the App Store since November 2013 and has 3,482 US ratings at 4.7 stars. Version 8, its biggest update, added collages and a video editor, and version 8.0.15 came out on 11 November 2026. Everything here comes from its App Store listing, checked on 21 November 2026, compared with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly.

What does MaxVault do well, and where does it fall short?

MaxVault does more than any other vault we've reviewed and is actively developed. Its gaps are encryption and privacy.

What MaxVault does well

  • Long-running and updated. On the App Store since 2013, with version 8.0.15 on 11 November 2026.
  • Layers of locks. A 4 to 8 digit PIN or Face ID for the app, plus a password or Face ID on individual folders.
  • Several vaults. Each opens with a different passcode, so one can hold harmless files.
  • Break-in alerts and a panic lock. It records an intruder's photo and GPS location, and locks when you turn the phone face down or swipe with three fingers.
  • Every file type. Live Photos, GIFs, PDFs, password-protected ZIPs, Office and iWork documents, and all VLC video and audio formats.
  • A private browser and downloader that saves straight into the vault, plus a scanner, photo and video editor, collages and duplicate finder.
  • Privacy tools. It can blur faces, redact details and remove photo metadata.
  • Cloud options. iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, iTunes sharing and Wi-Fi transfer.

Where MaxVault falls short

  • No encryption named. The listing describes locks, not encrypted files.
  • Cloud copies may not be protected. The listing doesn't say whether files sent to iCloud Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive are encrypted.
  • Data Linked to You. Its privacy label says some data is linked to your identity.
  • No recovery in the listing. It doesn't say what happens if you forget your PIN.
  • Expensive to own. Lifetime Premium is $79.99 or $99.99; the listing says to check the app for current options.

How are MaxVault and Noxium different?

MaxVault is a powerful file manager with locks. Noxium is a focused, encrypted photo vault with an encrypted backup.

Encryption

Noxium encrypts every photo, video and note on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your pattern through Argon2id; names, dates and places are encrypted too. MaxVault's listing doesn't mention encryption. See how encrypted photo vaults work for why that matters.

Backup

MaxVault syncs with iCloud Drive, Dropbox and OneDrive, but doesn't say whether those copies are encrypted. Noxium backs up only encrypted data to your own iCloud, free, and restores on a new iPhone when you draw your pattern; see encrypted iCloud backup.

Decoys and intruders

MaxVault's separate vaults and folder passwords can hide what matters, and its break-in alerts record who tried to get in. Noxium has a second pattern for a separate decoy vault, free, and Pro adds an emergency erase pattern.

Getting back in

MaxVault's listing doesn't describe recovery. Noxium can't reset your pattern for you; you write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault on this iPhone or a new one.

Features

MaxVault wins on breadth: browser, downloader, media player, editors, scanner and dozens of file types. Noxium sticks to photos, videos and encrypted notes, and keeps HEIC, ProRAW and Live Photos in full quality with their original dates.

Price

MaxVault Premium is $7.99 a month, $29.99 a year, or $79.99 or $99.99 for lifetime, and it also offers optional tips. Noxium's free plan includes 150 photos, 10 videos, unlimited notes and full backup; Pro is $2.99 a week, $17.99 a year or $39.99 once.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick MaxVault if you want one app for many file types, a private browser and downloader, editing tools, folder locks and break-in alerts.
  • Pick Noxium if you mainly protect photos and videos and want named encryption, an encrypted backup in your own iCloud, a recovery phrase and no ads, accounts or analytics.

How do you switch from MaxVault without losing photos?

Export the photos, check the copies, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything.

  1. Open each vault and locked folder and count what you have.
  2. Export photos and videos to your photo library or Files, or copy them to a computer with Wi-Fi transfer.
  3. Keep documents elsewhere. Noxium doesn't store PDFs, Office files or ZIPs, so move those to Files or another app.
  4. Check the copies: counts match, videos play, Live Photos move.
  5. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos. See import and export.
  6. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup.
  7. Only then delete MaxVault and any copies it synced to iCloud Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive, and cancel Premium in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in Photos or Files until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

Questions people ask
