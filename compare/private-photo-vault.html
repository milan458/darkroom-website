Private Photo Vault review: how it compares with Noxium

By Inertia Projects·Published 14 November 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Private Photo Vault (Pic Safe) is the most-reviewed vault on the US App Store, with about a million ratings, a break-in report, a decoy password and a private browser. If you forget its password, a recovery email you set up earlier is the way back in. Its encrypted Cloud Vault backup needs Premium and an account; the free version doesn't back up. Pick it for its features and track record; pick Noxium, which we make, for a free encrypted backup in your own iCloud and no data used to track you.

Private Photo Vault is a capable, long-running vault with more features than most, but its free version has no backup, and its privacy label lists data used to track you. If you want a free encrypted backup and no tracking, look elsewhere; if you want a break-in report, a private browser and a long track record, it's a strong pick. This review is for iPhone users who use Private Photo Vault, are locked out of it, or are deciding whether to start with it.

The app, also listed as Pic Safe, comes from Legendary Software Labs LLC and has about a million US ratings averaging 4.8 (App Store). This review uses its App Store page and its own website, privatephotovault.com, checked 28 November 2026, and compares it with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly.

What does Private Photo Vault do well, and where does it fall short?

It has more features than almost any vault and a long track record. Its gaps are in what's free, in the account its backup needs, and in what it tracks.

What Private Photo Vault does well

  • Track record. Still updated (version 30.1 came out in late November 2026), and rated 4.8 by about a million people.
  • Choice of lock. A PIN, a pattern (dot) lock or a password, plus separate passwords for individual albums.
  • Break-in report. The listing describes a photo plus the GPS location of break-in attempts. Noxium doesn't have this.
  • Decoy password and face-down lock, for when someone grabs the phone or makes you open it.
  • A Cloud Vault the company can't read. Its website says Cloud Vault encrypts files on your device, with a key made from your Cloud Vault Password, before uploading them, and that the password is never uploaded to its servers (privatephotovault.com).
  • A way back in. If you set up a recovery email when you first used the app, it can recover your app or album password (Private Photo Vault FAQ).
  • Extras: a private web browser that saves straight to the vault, wireless photo transfer, and apps for iPad, Mac and Apple Vision.

Where Private Photo Vault falls short

  • No backup on the free version. The listing says the free version doesn't back up your files to Cloud Vault. Lose the phone and the vault goes with it.
  • The backup needs Premium and an account. Its website says Cloud Vault "requires Premium, an account and internet". And if Premium expires and you choose Switch to Basic, the FAQ says your Cloud Backup will be deleted.
  • Its own cloud, not yours. Cloud Vault backups live with the developer, protected by your Cloud Vault Password. Forget that password and the listing says you can't view your Cloud Vault data; nobody can recover it for you.
  • No recovery email, no recovery. The FAQ says that without a recovery email, a forgotten app or album password can't be recovered through its process.
  • Tracking and ads. Its privacy label lists Usage Data under "Data Used to Track You", including for third-party advertising, and the listing says the app contains ads.
  • Unclear pricing. The listing shows Premium items at $6.99, $12.99, $35.99 and $39.99 without saying which is monthly, yearly or lifetime.

How are Private Photo Vault and Noxium different?

The biggest difference is the backup: Noxium's is free and goes to your own iCloud, while Private Photo Vault's needs Premium and an account and goes to its own cloud. After that, it's how you get back in when you forget your code, and what each app tracks.

How do you unlock Private Photo Vault if you forgot the password?

With the recovery email, if you set one up. Its FAQ gives the steps (Private Photo Vault FAQ):

  1. App password: enter it wrong 8 times in a row, then choose Yes when the recovery prompt appears.
  2. Album password: enter it wrong 5 times in a row, then choose Yes at the prompt.
  3. Don't delete the app to reset the password. The FAQ warns that files stored only in the app can be lost.

Without a recovery email, the FAQ says those passwords can't be recovered this way. The Cloud Vault Password is separate, and nothing recovers it: not support, not your app passcode, not your recovery email. If you're stuck, contact the developer through its support page before you try anything else. Our guide to forgotten vault passwords covers what to check for other apps.

Noxium works differently. There's no email and no reset by anyone, including us or Apple. You write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault. Lose both your pattern and the phrase and nobody can open the vault, which is also why nobody else can.

Backup and restore

Noxium backs up everything to your own private iCloud on the free plan, and only encrypted data leaves the phone. On a new iPhone you draw your pattern and the vault restores; see encrypted iCloud backup. If Noxium Pro ends, you keep full access to view, export and back up everything already in your vault. Private Photo Vault's Cloud Vault also encrypts with a secret only you know, which is good, but it needs Premium and an account, lives on the developer's servers, and is deleted if you switch to Basic.

Encryption on the phone

Both name AES-256. Noxium encrypts every photo, video and note on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your pattern through Argon2id. Names, dates and places are encrypted too, and the pattern is never stored. Private Photo Vault's FAQ says it uses AES-256 to protect your data, but the pages we read don't say how the key is made from your PIN. Our page on on-device encryption explains why that part matters.

Locks, decoys and alerts

Both offer a decoy. Private Photo Vault adds a break-in report, a face-down lock and per-album passwords. Noxium adds album patterns (Pro) and an emergency erase pattern (Pro), which opens a harmless vault and erases the others. A decoy only helps if it looks real; see what a decoy vault is.

Price and privacy

Noxium Pro is $2.99 a week, $17.99 a year or $39.99 once (our pricing), and the free plan includes 150 photos, 10 videos and full backup. Noxium has no ads, no accounts and no analytics; its privacy label is Data Not Collected.

Which should you choose?

Choose by what matters more: features or a free backup with no tracking.

  • Pick Private Photo Vault if you want a break-in report, a private browser, a Mac app, a long track record, or an iPhone on iOS 15 or 16. Set up a recovery email on day one.
  • Pick Noxium if you want a free encrypted backup in your own iCloud, encryption that's named and documented end to end, a recovery phrase instead of an email, and no data used to track you.

What neither protects: photos still in your Photos library before you import them, copies you've already sent, and a code someone watched you enter.

How do you switch from Private Photo Vault without losing photos?

Export to the Photos app, check the copies, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything. Private Photo Vault's listing says it can import from and export to the Photos app.

  1. Count what you have in each album, including any album with its own password and the decoy.
  2. Get the originals onto the phone. If you use Cloud Vault with Space Saver, the originals are in Cloud Vault. Make sure the full-size files are on the phone before you export, and do it before Premium ends.
  3. Export each album to Photos from inside Private Photo Vault.
  4. Check the copies in Photos: counts match, videos play, photos are full size.
  5. Import into Noxium. With Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, it offers to remove the copies from Photos. Then empty Recently Deleted.
  6. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup, and only then delete from Private Photo Vault and cancel Premium in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Photos you export are unencrypted in your library until the new vault imports them. Move them in one sitting, on a phone only you are using. More on this in moving a vault without losing photos.

Questions people ask

How do I unlock Private Photo Vault if I forgot my password?

Use the recovery email, if you set one up. Enter the app password wrong 8 times in a row (5 for an album) and choose Yes at the recovery prompt. Without a recovery email, its FAQ says the password can't be recovered this way. Don't delete the app to reset it.

What happens if I forget my Cloud Vault Password?

You can't view your Cloud Vault data. The password is never uploaded to its servers, and its website says neither support, your app passcode nor your recovery email can recover it. Write it down somewhere safe.

Does Private Photo Vault back up my photos?

Only with Premium. The free version doesn't back up to Cloud Vault, and Cloud Vault needs an account. If Premium ends and you switch to Basic, its FAQ says the cloud backup is deleted.

Is Private Photo Vault safe?

It's a long-running, actively updated app that says it uses AES-256, with a Cloud Vault the company says it can't read. The free version has no backup, and its privacy label lists data used to track you. See are photo vault apps safe?

Is Private Photo Vault the same as Keepsafe?

No. They're different apps from different companies. Keepsafe signs you in with an email account; our Keepsafe review compares it with Noxium.
