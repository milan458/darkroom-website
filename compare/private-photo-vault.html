Private Photo Vault review: how it compares with Noxium

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 14 November 2026

Short answer

Private Photo Vault (Pic Safe) is the most-reviewed vault on the US App Store, with over a million ratings, a break-in report, a decoy password and a private browser. Its encrypted Cloud Vault backup is a Premium feature in its own cloud; the free version doesn't back up. Pick it for its features and track record; pick Noxium if you want a free encrypted backup in your own iCloud and no data used to track you.

Private Photo Vault, from Legendary Software Labs, has been on the App Store since February 2011. With 1,019,778 US ratings at 4.8 stars, it's the most familiar name in photo vaults. This review covers what it does well and where it falls short, from its App Store listing checked on 14 November 2026, and compares it with Noxium. What does Private Photo Vault do well, and where does it fall short?

It has more features than almost any vault and a 15-year track record. Its gaps are in what's free and in what the listing says about encryption on the phone.

What Private Photo Vault does well

  • Track record. On the App Store since 2011, still updated (version 30.0 came out on 12 November 2026), and rated 4.8 by over a million people.
  • Choice of lock. A PIN or a pattern, plus a separate password for individual albums.
  • Break-in report. The listing describes a photo plus GPS location of break-in attempts. Noxium doesn't have this.
  • Decoy password and face-down lock, for when someone grabs the phone or makes you open it.
  • A Cloud Vault that the company can't read. The listing says your Cloud Vault Password is never backed up to its servers and is what encrypts your files, so its employees can't view them.
  • Extras: a private web browser that saves straight to the vault, wireless transfer, iTunes syncing, and iPad support.

Where Private Photo Vault falls short

  • No backup on the free version. The listing says so directly and asks free users to back up their own photos. Lose the phone and the vault goes with it.
  • Encryption on the phone isn't described. The listing describes the Cloud Vault as encrypted but says nothing about how files stored on the iPhone are protected, beyond the passcode.
  • Its own cloud, not yours. Cloud Vault backups live with the developer, protected by your Cloud Vault Password. Forget that password and the listing says you can't view your Cloud Vault data.
  • Tracking. Its privacy label lists data used to track you and data linked to you.
  • Unclear pricing. The listing shows Premium purchases from $6.99 to $39.99 without saying which is which.

How are Private Photo Vault and Noxium different?

The biggest difference is the backup: Noxium's is free and goes to your own iCloud, while Private Photo Vault's is Premium and goes to its own cloud. After that, it's how much each says about encryption, and what each tracks.

Backup and restore

Noxium backs up everything to your own private iCloud on the free plan, and only encrypted data leaves the phone. On a new iPhone you draw your pattern and the vault restores; see encrypted iCloud backup. Private Photo Vault's Cloud Vault also encrypts with a secret only you know, which is good, but it needs Premium and lives on the developer's servers.

Encryption on the phone

Noxium encrypts every photo, video and note on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your pattern through Argon2id. Names, dates and places are encrypted too, and the pattern is never stored. Private Photo Vault's listing names no cipher and doesn't describe encryption of the files on the phone. Our page on on-device encryption explains why that part matters.

Locks, decoys and alerts

Both offer a pattern lock, a decoy and a face-down lock. Private Photo Vault adds a break-in report and per-album passwords. Noxium adds album patterns (Pro) and an emergency erase pattern (Pro), which opens a harmless vault and erases the others.

Forgetting your code

Noxium can't be reset by anyone. You write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault on this iPhone or a new one. Private Photo Vault's listing warns that a forgotten Cloud Vault Password means losing access to that cloud data; it doesn't describe recovery for the app lock itself.

Price and privacy

Noxium Pro is $2.99 a week, $17.99 a year or $39.99 once, and the free plan includes 150 photos, 10 videos and full backup. Noxium has no ads, no accounts and no analytics; its privacy label is Data Not Collected.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Private Photo Vault if you want a break-in report, a private browser, a long track record, or an iPhone on iOS 15 or 16.
  • Pick Noxium if you want a free encrypted backup in your own iCloud, encryption on the phone that's named and documented, a recovery phrase, and no data used to track you.

How do you switch from Private Photo Vault without losing photos?

Export to the Photos app, check the copies, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything. Private Photo Vault's listing says it can import from and export to the Photos app.

  1. Count what you have in each album, including any album with its own password and the decoy.
  2. Export each album to Photos from inside Private Photo Vault. If you use Cloud Vault with Space Saver, make sure the full-size originals are on the phone first.
  3. Check the copies in Photos: counts match, videos play, photos are full size.
  4. Import into Noxium. With Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, it offers to remove the copies from Photos. Then empty Recently Deleted.
  5. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup.
  6. Only then delete from Private Photo Vault and cancel Premium in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Photos you export are unencrypted in your library until the new vault imports them. Move them in one sitting, on a phone only you are using. More on this in moving a vault without losing photos.

Questions people ask

Is Private Photo Vault safe?

It's a long-running, actively updated app with a Cloud Vault the company says it can't read. Its listing doesn't describe encryption of files on the phone, the free version has no backup, and its privacy label lists data used to track you. See are photo vault apps safe?

Does Private Photo Vault back up my photos?

Only with Premium. Its listing says the free version doesn't back up to Cloud Vault and asks you to keep your own backups.

What is the break-in report?

Its listing describes it as a photo plus GPS location recorded when someone tries to get in with the wrong code, so you can see who tried and where.

What happens if I forget my Cloud Vault Password?

Its listing says you won't be able to view your Cloud Vault data, because the password is never stored on its servers. Write it down somewhere safe.

Is Private Photo Vault the same as Keepsafe?

No. They're different apps from different companies. Our Keepsafe review compares that one with Noxium.
