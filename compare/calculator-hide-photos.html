Its listing doesn't name any encryption, and the free version has ads. Pick Calculator# for its disguise and extras; pick Noxium if you want named encryption and no ads or tracking.

What does Calculator# do well, and where does it fall short?

Calculator# does more than almost any vault, at a low price. What its listing doesn't say is how any of it is protected.

What Calculator# does well

  • A convincing disguise. It works as a calculator, so a quick look at the phone shows nothing unusual.
  • Many lock options: a calculator code, PIN, password, pattern, or Touch ID / Face ID.
  • Far more than photos. Documents, audio, notes, to-dos, secret contacts and a password manager and wallet, all in one place.
  • Backup to the cloud you choose: iCloud, Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive.
  • Email recovery if you forget your password.
  • Hack Monitor logs wrong password attempts, a panic switch jumps to another app, and a disguise mode shows a fake crash message.
  • Cheap. $4.99 a year removes ads and limits. It's actively updated (30 October 2026) and works on iPad.

Where Calculator# falls short

  • No encryption named. The listing talks about hiding, locking and password-protecting, but never says how files are encrypted, or whether the cloud backups are.
  • A disguise isn't encryption. A calculator front keeps out a casual glance. It does nothing if someone gets at the files another way, or knows what the app is.
  • No decoy space. The panic switch jumps to another app, which helps against someone looking over your shoulder but not someone who makes you open the vault.
  • Ads and tracking. The free version shows ads, and its privacy label lists data used to track you.
  • Email recovery cuts both ways. It's a safety net, but anyone with access to that email account may be able to use it too.

How are Calculator# and Noxium different?

Calculator# hides things behind a disguise and many features. Noxium does fewer things and encrypts all of them with a key only your pattern makes.

Encryption

Noxium encrypts every photo, video and note on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your pattern through Argon2id. Names, dates and places are encrypted too. Calculator#'s listing names no cipher. If that matters to you, ask the developer; our guide on how encrypted photo vaults work explains what to ask.

Backup

Calculator# lets you back up to iCloud, Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive. Its listing doesn't say whether those backups are encrypted first. Noxium backs up only encrypted data, to your own iCloud, and restores on a new iPhone with your pattern; see encrypted iCloud backup.

Disguise

Calculator# is a working calculator. Noxium Pro can change its Home Screen icon to a Calculator, Notes or Weather lookalike, and hides vault content in the app switcher and in screen recordings, but it opens to its own lock screen, not a working calculator.

When someone makes you open it

Noxium's second pattern opens a separate decoy vault, 1 on the free plan, and Pro adds an emergency erase pattern. Calculator#'s listing offers a panic switch and a fake crash message instead. See what a decoy vault is.

Price and privacy

Calculator#'s yearly plan is $4.99. Noxium's free plan has no ads and holds 150 photos, 10 videos and unlimited notes, with full backup; Noxium Pro is $17.99 a year or $39.99 once. Noxium has no ads, no accounts and no analytics, and its privacy label is Data Not Collected.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick Calculator# if a working calculator disguise matters most, or you want one app for photos, documents, passwords and contacts, with backup to Dropbox or Google Drive.
  • Pick Noxium if you want encryption that's named and documented, an encrypted backup in your own iCloud, a decoy vault, and no ads or tracking.

How do you switch from Calculator# without losing photos?

Move the photos back to your Photos library, check them, import them into the new vault, and only then delete them from Calculator#.

  1. Count what you have in each Calculator# album, and note any documents or passwords you'll need to move elsewhere.
  2. Get the photos and videos out of Calculator#, back to your Photos library from inside the app, or to a computer with the Wi-Fi transfer its listing describes.
  3. Check them in Photos: counts match, videos play, photos open at full size.
  4. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos.
  5. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup.
  6. Only then delete from Calculator#, remove any cloud backups you made with it, and cancel the subscription in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in your Photos library until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

Encryption, not just a disguise

Noxium encrypts everything on your iPhone with AES-256-GCM. Pro can also change its icon to a Calculator, Notes or Weather lookalike.

Questions people ask

Is Calculator# Hide Photos safe?

It hides files behind a working calculator and a code, and it's actively updated. Its listing doesn't name any encryption, and its privacy label lists data used to track you. See are photo vault apps safe?

How much does Calculator# cost?

It's free with ads and limits. A year without ads costs $4.99 on the US App Store, and a Premium Features Pack is another $4.99.

What if I forget my Calculator# password?

Its listing says you can recover it through the email address you registered. Make sure that email account is secure, because it's a way into the vault.

Do calculator vault apps really hide photos?

They hide the app's purpose from a quick look. Whether the photos are protected depends on encryption, which a disguise doesn't provide by itself. Many vaults, including Noxium Pro, offer a lookalike icon on top of encryption.

Does Calculator# back up my photos?

Yes, if you set it up. Its listing says it can back up to iCloud, Dropbox, Google Drive or OneDrive.
