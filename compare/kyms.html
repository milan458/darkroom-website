KYMS review: how it compares with Noxium

By Inertia Projects·Last updated 22 November 2026

Short answer

KYMS (Secret photos - KYMS) is a long-running, well-rated vault for photos, videos, documents and passwords, with AES encryption, a fake PIN for a decoy vault, a panic gesture and a private browser. Its listing describes no backup or recovery, it shows ads unless you pay to remove them, and its privacy label includes data used to track you. Pick KYMS for file types and extras; pick Noxium for no ads or tracking and an encrypted iCloud backup.

Secret photos - KYMS, by IdeaSolutions S.r.l., has been on the App Store since October 2011 and has 17,322 US ratings at 4.7 stars. It's one of the oldest vault apps still updated: version 3.6.7 came out on 18 November 2026. Everything here comes from its App Store listing, checked on 22 November 2026, compared with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly.

What does KYMS do well, and where does it fall short?

KYMS encrypts, has a decoy, and handles almost any file. Its gaps are backup, ads and tracking.

What KYMS does well

  • Encryption, named in part. The listing says files are protected with AES, which is more than many vault apps say.
  • A decoy vault. A fake PIN opens a separate, harmless vault.
  • A panic gesture. Cover the screen with your hand or close a smart cover and KYMS locks.
  • Layered login. You can combine a PIN, an alphanumeric password and Face ID or Touch ID.
  • Plays almost anything. FLV, AVI, WMV and MKV videos play while they're still being decrypted, and it opens and edits PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Pages and Numbers files.
  • Lots of extras. A private browser with tabs and video download, a password vault, and with Premium a contacts vault, audio recorder, card wallet, document scanner and task manager.
  • Long track record. On the App Store since 2011 and still updated in November 2026.

Where KYMS falls short

  • No backup described. The listing doesn't say how to back up the vault or move it to a new iPhone.
  • No recovery described. It doesn't say what happens if you forget your PIN or password.
  • Ads and tracking. Ads stay unless you pay $4.99, and the privacy label lists Data Used to Track You.
  • Encryption details are thin. "AES military-grade" doesn't say how keys are made from your PIN or where they're kept.
  • Prices are unclear. The description mentions subscriptions, but the US purchase list shows only two items at $4.99 each.

How are KYMS and Noxium different?

Both encrypt and both have a decoy. The differences are backup, recovery, ads and how much each app does.

Encryption

Noxium encrypts every photo, video and note on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your pattern through Argon2id; names, dates and places are encrypted too. KYMS says AES without the details. Both are better than a PIN screen alone. See how encrypted photo vaults work for what the details mean.

Backup

KYMS's listing describes no backup. Noxium backs up only encrypted data to your own iCloud, free, and restores on a new iPhone when you draw your pattern; see encrypted iCloud backup.

Decoys and emergencies

KYMS has a fake PIN for a decoy vault and a panic gesture that locks the app. Noxium has a second pattern that opens a separate decoy vault, free, and Pro adds an emergency erase pattern.

Getting back in

KYMS's listing doesn't describe recovery. Noxium can't reset your pattern for you; you write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault on this iPhone or a new one.

Features

KYMS does much more: documents, passwords, a browser, contacts, recordings and a wallet. Noxium sticks to photos, videos and encrypted notes, and keeps HEIC, ProRAW and Live Photos in full quality.

Price

KYMS lists "Remove advertising" and "Premium features" at $4.99 each. Noxium's free plan includes 150 photos, 10 videos, unlimited notes and full backup, with no ads; Pro is $2.99 a week, $17.99 a year or $39.99 once.

Which should you choose?

  • Pick KYMS if you want one encrypted app for photos, videos, documents and passwords, with a decoy PIN, a panic gesture and a private browser.
  • Pick Noxium if you mainly protect photos and videos and want an encrypted backup that survives a lost phone, a recovery phrase, and no ads or tracking.

How do you switch from KYMS without losing photos?

Export the photos, check the copies, import them into the new vault, and only then delete anything.

  1. Count what you have in each KYMS album, and in the decoy vault if you want to keep it.
  2. Export photos and videos to your photo library or Files from inside KYMS.
  3. Move documents and passwords to Files and a password manager. Noxium doesn't store them.
  4. Check the copies: counts match, videos play, photos open at full size.
  5. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos on, then empty Recently Deleted in Photos. See import and export.
  6. Check the backup in Noxium's Settings › iCloud backup.
  7. Only then delete KYMS.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in Photos or Files until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

Encrypted, backed up, no ads

Noxium encrypts every photo on your iPhone and backs up only ciphertext to your own iCloud. No ads, no account, no analytics.

Questions people ask

Is KYMS safe?

It says it encrypts with AES and has a decoy PIN and a panic lock. Its listing doesn't describe a backup or recovery, and its privacy label includes data used to track you. See are photo vault apps safe?

Does KYMS have a fake PIN?

Yes. Its listing says a fake PIN opens a decoy vault. See what a decoy vault is and how to test one.

Does KYMS have ads?

Yes, unless you buy "Remove advertising", $4.99 on the US App Store in November 2026.

What does KYMS stand for?

The listing doesn't say. The app's full App Store name is "Secret photos - KYMS".

Does KYMS back up to iCloud?

Its listing doesn't describe a backup. Export important photos before you change or reset your iPhone.
