Keepsafe review: how it compares with Noxium in 2026

By Inertia Projects·Published 14 November 2026·Last updated 28 November 2026

Short answer

Keepsafe is a mature, actively updated vault that encrypts files with 256-bit AES and syncs through an email account and its own Keepsafe cloud. The free plan shows ads and backs up your first 200 items; Premium adds a decoy vault (fake PIN) and backs up up to 10,000. Pick Keepsafe if you want email sign-in and Android; pick Noxium, which we make, if you want no account, a backup encrypted in your own iCloud and a one-time price.

Keepsafe is a safe, well-maintained photo vault if you're comfortable with two things: an email account you sign in with, and a copy of your vault in Keepsafe's own cloud. If you want neither, a vault with no account fits you better. This review is for iPhone users deciding whether to start with Keepsafe, stay on it, or leave it. It uses Keepsafe's US App Store page and its help center, checked 28 November 2026, and compares it with Noxium. We make Noxium, so weigh what we say about our own app accordingly.

Keepsafe has been around a long time and it shows: about 387,000 US ratings averaging 4.7, and version 13.36.0 out on 12 November 2026 (App Store). Already decided to leave? What does Keepsafe do well, and where does it fall short?

Keepsafe is mature, well designed and works across platforms. Its trade-offs come from the account, the company cloud and the ads.

What Keepsafe does well

  • It's actively maintained. Version 13.36.0 came out on 12 November 2026, and the app has a large, long-running user base (App Store).
  • It works on Android too. The listing says your items sync to all your Keepsafe installations on any platform, and the help center gives export steps for both iOS and Android (Keepsafe Help Center). That's rare among vaults.
  • Its encryption is named. The help center says all files are encrypted at rest with 256-bit AES and that backups are encrypted (Keepsafe Help Center).
  • A decoy vault, now documented. With Premium, a fake PIN opens a second, separate gallery you can show someone instead of your real one (Keepsafe Help Center). Premium also adds intruder alerts and trash recovery (Keepsafe Subscriptions).
  • A Lifetime plan. The help center lists Monthly, Yearly and Lifetime plans, and Lifetime backs up up to 100,000 items.
  • Useful extras. Album Lock gives single albums their own PIN (Premium), and Space Saver keeps compressed copies on the phone and the originals in the cloud, which frees space on a full iPhone.

Where Keepsafe falls short

  • The account is the only key to your cloud copy. Keepsafe signs you in by sending an access code to your verified email. Its help center says that if you permanently lose access to that email, the account and its contents can't be recovered, and it suggests adding a second email address (Keepsafe Help Center).
  • Your backup lives with Keepsafe. It goes to "your own private Keepsafe cloud", not to your iCloud (Keepsafe Help Center). The help pages we read don't say who holds the encryption keys for that cloud copy.
  • The free backup is small. Basic backs up your first 200 items. Items you move to the Trash still count toward that limit until you empty it.
  • Ads and tracking on the free plan. The listing says Keepsafe Basic is ad supported, and its privacy label lists Usage Data under "Data Used to Track You".
  • Unclear Lifetime price. The listing labels only Monthly Premium ($9.99) and Yearly Premium ($23.99). Its other Premium items run from $4.99 to $35.99 without saying which one is Lifetime, so check the purchase screen before you pay.
  • Space Saver's catch. With it on, the full-size originals exist only in Keepsafe's cloud. Leaving later means getting the originals back first.

How are Keepsafe and Noxium different?

The main difference is who holds your account and your backup. With Keepsafe, the company does. With Noxium, there is no account, and the backup sits in your own iCloud, encrypted with a key only your pattern makes.

What is Keepsafe Cloud, and how is it different from iCloud backup?

Keepsafe Cloud, also called Private Cloud, is Keepsafe's own backup. It mirrors the gallery on your phone, is tied to your Keepsafe account, and is turned on or off in Settings › Backup & Sync inside the app (Keepsafe Help Center). Keepsafe says that if your phone is lost or replaced, only the synced items come back. The limits are 200 items free, 10,000 with Premium and 100,000 with Lifetime.

Noxium backs up everything, free, to your own private iCloud, and only encrypted data leaves the phone. On a new iPhone you draw your pattern and the vault restores. See encrypted iCloud backup for what's uploaded and how restore works. What it doesn't protect: the backup opens only with your pattern or recovery phrase, so if you lose both, nobody can restore it, not us and not Apple.

Account and recovery

Keepsafe's email account gives you a way back in on a new phone, as long as you still have that email. Noxium has no account and no reset by anyone, including us or Apple. Instead, you write down a recovery phrase at setup, and it opens your vault if you forget your pattern. Lose both and nobody can open the vault, which is also why nobody else can. Our guide to forgotten vault passwords compares how each kind of vault handles this.

Encryption

Both say they use AES-256. Noxium names the whole chain: Argon2id turns your pattern into a key, every file is sealed with AES-256-GCM on the iPhone, and names, dates and places are encrypted too. Keepsafe's help center names 256-bit AES for files at rest and for backups. With Keepsafe, a copy of your files also lives on the company's servers, tied to your account.

Decoys and lock

Both now have a decoy. In Keepsafe, turn it on in Settings › Decoy Vault: switch the toggle on, confirm your real PIN, then create a decoy PIN (Keepsafe Help Center). It's a Premium feature. Noxium opens with a pattern across a 5×5 plate, and a second pattern opens a separate decoy vault, 1 on the free plan. A decoy only helps if it looks lived in; see what a decoy vault is and what it can't hide.

Price and privacy

Over a year, Keepsafe Premium is $23.99 on the US App Store. Noxium Pro is $17.99 a year, or $39.99 once (our pricing). Keepsafe has a Lifetime plan too, but its listing doesn't say which price it is. Noxium has no ads and no analytics, and its privacy label is Data Not Collected.

Which should you choose?

Choose by whether you want an account and a company cloud.

  • Pick Keepsafe if you use Android too, you want an email account to sign in with on a new phone, or you want Space Saver to free up phone storage. Keep a second email on the account.
  • Pick Noxium if you don't want an account or a company cloud, you want your backup in your own iCloud, encrypted, and a decoy on the free plan.

What no vault protects, Noxium and Keepsafe included: photos still sitting in your Photos library before you import them, copies you've already sent someone, and a code someone watched you enter.

How do you switch from Keepsafe without losing photos?

Export from Keepsafe to your Photos library, check everything arrived at full quality, import it into the new vault, and only then delete Keepsafe's copies.

  1. Mind Space Saver. If you use it, the originals are in Keepsafe's cloud. Export a few photos first and check they're full size before you go on.
  2. Export album by album. Keepsafe's help center describes it: open an album, tap Select, choose the items, tap the send icon at the bottom left, and choose Export to Camera Roll (Keepsafe Help Center).
  3. Check the export in Photos: counts match, videos play, and a few photos open at full size. Exported photos return to the date they were taken, so look further back in your library.
  4. Import into Noxium with Settings › App settings › Offer to delete from Photos turned on. Then empty Recently Deleted in Photos.
  5. Check Noxium's backup in Settings › iCloud backup.
  6. Only then delete items from Keepsafe and cancel Premium in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.
Watch out

Exported photos sit unencrypted in your Photos library until the new vault imports them. Do the move in one sitting, on a phone only you are using.

Questions people ask

Is Keepsafe Photo Vault safe?

Yes, for most people. It's a long-running, actively updated app, and its help center says files are encrypted at rest with 256-bit AES. The trade-offs are that your account and your cloud copy sit with Keepsafe, losing your email can lock you out for good, and the free plan shows ads. Our checklist: are photo vault apps safe?

What is Keepsafe Cloud?

It's Keepsafe's own encrypted backup, tied to your Keepsafe account and stored with Keepsafe, not in your iCloud. It holds 200 items on the free plan, 10,000 with Premium and 100,000 with Lifetime. Turn it on or off in Settings › Backup & Sync.

Does Keepsafe have a fake PIN or decoy vault?

Yes, with Premium. Keepsafe calls it Decoy Vault: a second PIN opens a separate gallery. Turn it on in Settings › Decoy Vault.

Is Keepsafe free, and is there a lifetime plan?

It has a free, ad-supported plan that backs up your first 200 items. Premium costs $9.99 a month or $23.99 a year on the US App Store, checked 28 November 2026. The help center also lists a Lifetime plan, but the listing doesn't say which price it is.

What is a good Keepsafe alternative?

If the account is what bothers you, pick a vault that needs none and backs up to your own iCloud, such as Vaultaire or Noxium, which we make. Our Keepsafe alternatives guide compares the options and their prices.

How do I get my photos out of Keepsafe?

Open an album, tap Select, pick the items, tap the send icon and choose Export to Camera Roll. If you use Space Saver, check that the exported photos are full size.
